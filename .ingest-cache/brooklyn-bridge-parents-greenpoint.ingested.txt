content.rendered: Brooklyn’s biggest stories of the week: The City banned AI for students through eighth grade, and announced it in Brooklyn; $43 million moved to keep 3-K and pre-K programs open; Carnival weekend filled the Brooklyn Museum grounds; the protected bike corridor from Sunset Park to DUMBO was finally finished; Landmarks approved 61 apartments inside the Hotel Bossert; a taqueria and coffee shop opened in Bed-Stuy; and the jellyfish arrived at the Rockaways. Read on for the latest! Schools & Childcare New York City bans AI for students through eighth grade Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Chancellor Kamar Samuels announced a one-year moratorium on student-facing generative AI from 3-K through eighth grade on Wednesday, at the Brooklyn STEAM Center in the Navy Yard. It covers roughly 600,000 children, two-thirds of the system. Companion chatbots are banned at every grade. The full policy is here . Screen time now has a number attached to it The same announcement capped recommended screen time at 30 minutes a day in grades 3 through 5 and 45 minutes in grades 6 through 8, and ended one-to-one devices in second grade and below. Students with disabilities, multilingual learners and career-and-technical students are exempt. Teachers may still use AI to plan. The city moved $43 million to keep 3-K and pre-K programs open After early-childhood providers warned they might not open on time, the city announced an overhaul of its payment system on Monday: $43 million, staff reassigned to speed contract registration, and interest-free loans. It answers the funding gap we wrote about last week . Details here . Public school starts Thursday, September 10 Teachers return Tuesday the 8th for two prep days; the first day for students is Thursday, September 10. Yom Kippur closes schools Monday, September 21, and evening parent-teacher conferences begin September 23. Events & Entertainment Robyn plays her first arena tour at Barclays Center The Swedish pop singer brought the Sexistential Tour to Barclays on Friday night, her first time playing arenas, with Yaeji and Romy opening. A second Brooklyn date follows on the 10th, and a dollar from every ticket goes to LGBTQ+ youth and health organizations. Tickets and details . Carnival weekend took over the Brooklyn Museum grounds Blocko Mania ran Thursday and Soca Fest Friday, and Saturday brought the two events families actually plan around: Junior Carnival, which assembled at 9am at St. John’s Place and Kingston Avenue and costumed masqueraders up to age 17, and the Panorama steel band finals, gates at 6pm. The parade itself steps off on Eastern Parkway on Labor Day Monday. The official schedule is here . Pete Davidson spent the week filming in Bay Ridge and Coney Island Davidson has been shooting a mob picture around southern Brooklyn for about a month, playing Bonanno-family figure Thomas Pitera opposite Paul Walter Hauser, Camila Mendes and Simon Rex. He graduated from Xaverian High School in Bay Ridge in 2011, so the location scouting more or less picked itself. City Hall & Transit The Sunset Park to DUMBO bike corridor is finally finished The city completed the last block of protected lane on Ashland Place on Tuesday, closing the final gap in a continuous protected route from Sunset Park to DUMBO. The Downtown Brooklyn stretch was nicknamed Crashland; work began in 2022 and sat unfinished for three years. A two-way bike path on the widened south sidewalk of Lafayette Avenue carries riders across Flatbush. The announcement is here . Three Crown Heights buildings were taken from a negligent owner The city announced on Thursday that three buildings holding 88 rent-stabilized homes had been transferred away from a landlord who had appeared on the worst-landlord list, with HPD overseeing repairs under the new owner. City Hall press releases are here . What closed, and what is closing Every Brooklyn Public Library branch was shut Saturday through Labor Day Monday. Mini pools close after Labor Day, so this was the last full weekend for the toddler pools; the larger outdoor pools and the lifeguarded beaches both run through September 13. Police Blotter The NYPD called it the safest summer on record for shootings and murders Commissioner Jessica Tisch announced on Thursday that major crime citywide fell 6.6% in August, with no shootings, murders or rapes in the subway system all month. Felony assault was the one category that rose. The release breaks out other boroughs but publishes no Brooklyn table. The figures are here . Police shot and killed a man on the Brooklyn Bridge on Friday morning A man climbed the bridge cables around 4:15am, said he had a bomb and wanted to die, and after an hour-long standoff came within a few feet of an officer with a knife. Brooklyn-bound lanes closed and reopened by 8:35am. amNewYork has the account . Police are still looking for a man who assaulted a woman in Park Slope A man came up behind a 38-year-old woman near 15th Street and Fifth Avenue just after 3am on Saturday the 29th, pushed her down and sexually assaulted her. He was a stranger to her, the attack was captured on camera, and officers spent the week canvassing with fliers. The police appeal is here . A nurse was killed in a Sunset Park hit-and-run, and officials want Third Avenue redesigned Jiayi Zheng, 28, an NYU Langone nurse walking home from an overnight shift, was struck at 55th Street and Third Avenue by a driver who fled. It is the eleventh pedestrian or cyclist death on that two-mile stretch under the BQE since 2014, and elected officials spent the week pressing for a redesign. NY1 reported it . A man was beaten to death on Grattan Street in Bushwick Police responded early Friday and found the man unconscious; he was pronounced dead at the scene. No arrests had been announced as the week closed. Real Estate & Development Landmarks approves 61 apartments inside the Hotel Bossert The Landmarks Preservation Commission voted 9-0 on Tuesday to approve rooftop and courtyard additions at 98 Montague Street, clearing the way for 61 condominiums aimed at three- and four-bedroom buyers, plus a ground-floor restaurant that would be Danny Meyer’s first full-service Brooklyn room. The revised scheme set the top two floors further back and darkened the cladding. Completion is targeted for 2028. The commission’s agenda records the vote . A new developer wants four 10-story buildings at the Arrow Linen site Goose Property Management has replaced Apex at 467 Prospect Avenue and is proposing 396 apartments across four ten-story buildings, against the two buildings and roughly 250 units neighbors negotiated in the approved rezoning. About a quarter would be affordable. Renderings show the massing pressed up against neighboring townhouses. Curbed asks where the Italians of Carroll Gardens went Three century-old Italian institutions changed hands or closed inside a year: Sam’s, open since 1930, sold in April; Ferdinando’s Focacceria, 104 years old, sold to Sal Lamboglia of Cafe Spaghetti; and Caputo’s Bake Shop closed this spring after 124 years. Italian ancestry across the surrounding neighborhoods is down about a quarter in 13 years. Curbed has the piece . Openings & Closings Peral opened in Bed-Stuy The taqueria and coffee shop at 105 Saratoga Avenue opened properly on Wednesday after a run of August previews, from the teams behind Olmo in Bed-Stuy and Cicatriz in Mexico City. The cafe runs mornings into mid-afternoon and the taco and bar service Wednesday through Sunday evenings. Hours are on their site . Kirbee’s fired up Texas barbecue in Greenpoint The 55 McGuinness Boulevard South barbecue joint soft-opened on Sunday the 30th and went to full service over the holiday weekend. It comes from pitmasters Chuck Charnichart of Barbs B Q in Lockhart and Jonny White of Goldee’s in Fort Worth. Walk-up window ordering, 24 seats. Their Instagram is the place to check hours. Sam’s returns to Court Street on September 10 The Cobble Hill Italian-American room that opened in 1930 reopens Thursday under Georgia Fulton, who spent 12 years at Long Island Bar, with the red vinyl booths and checkered tablecloths intact. Bookings are already live. Sam’s own site has the details. Around Brooklyn Jellyfish swarmed the Rockaways Mauve stingers that normally stay offshore moved in on the winds, and the Park Service posted warning signs at Riis and Fort Tilden. One Rockaway lifeguard said he had heard more jellyfish complaints in three days than in 25 years on the job. A South Slope lawyer stung on the forearm needed urgent care twice. Vinegar, salt water and a hot compress; not tap water. The City Reporter has the story . Whale sharks surfaced off a Sheepshead Bay boat Two whale sharks came up feet from an American Princess pelagic trip out of Sheepshead Bay on Monday, about 100 miles out at the Hudson Canyon. They are endangered filter feeders, harmless to people, and had not been documented in New York waters in decades. CBS New York reported it . The stained-glass watertower over Brooklyn Bridge Park came down Tom Fruin and his crew dismantled Watertower 3, the plexiglas-and-steel piece that has sat atop 334 Furman Street since 2014 and is visible from the Promenade and the BQE, on Tuesday in the rain. It is coming back. The building is being renovated and Fruin is repairing a decade of sun and storms; the tower returns in the fall of 2027. Sports On the Court The Liberty are on pause. The best of them is Swamp Dragons Night on January 29, honoring the name change New Jersey had approved, designed and very nearly adopted in 1995 before an owner changed his mind at the vote. Thursday brought the highlight of the year anyway: Ronald Hernandez went 5-for-5 with two home runs, six RBI and a stolen base in a 12-5 win. The season ends at home in Coney Island on Sunday afternoon. Schedule here . Peter Mangione made the USL Championship Team of the Week, and forward Markus Anderson was recalled from loan by the Philadelphia Union. content.rendered: August was a rather slow month in terms of new amenities for families in Brooklyn, however here are the new openings: A fourth Treasure Trunk Theatre studio in the South Slope, a free equipment-lending activity center on Governors Island, a full-size Lidl on Grand Street in Williamsburg, a brand-new Pilates studio in Bay Ridge, and — one bridge away — the Rockaways’ landmark Riis Park bathhouse, reborn as a beach club. Further out: the Brooklyn Conservatory of Music opens a second Park Slope location in September, and the Carroll Gardens and Red Hook libraries are both due back. New Lots Interim Library and Adult Learning Center | East New York | NOW OPEN Brooklyn Public Library opened an interim New Lots branch on August 12 on the campus of St. Paul Community Baptist Church, 818 Schenck Avenue, so the neighborhood keeps library service while the permanent building is rebuilt. The interim branch carries the full range of services, including books, computers and programming, and the adult learning center alongside it runs free classes in reading and writing, high school equivalency prep and English practice. IKEA donated the furnishings. The new library, designed by MASS Design Group and Marble Fairbanks, is expected to open in 2028. The branch is open seven days a week. Treasure Trunk Theatre | South Slope | NOW OPEN Treasure Trunk Theatre has opened a fourth Brooklyn studio, at 408 7th Avenue between 13th and 14th Streets. The children’s theatre company, founded by a local mother of two, has been running process-based drama programmes in the borough for thirteen years: camps for ages 3–12 on school holidays, an after-school club for 3–7s with pick-up from walkable schools and optional extended day to 6pm, baby and toddler classes from birth, a gentle pre-preschool programme for ages 1.5–3, birthday parties and full-scale musicals for older kids. New families can book a free trial class. The other three studios are at 141 Atlantic Avenue in Cobble Hill, 700 Washington Avenue in Prospect Heights and 179 4th Avenue in Park Slope. Governors Island Activity Center | Governors Island | NOW OPEN Governors Island has opened a free activity center at the Play Lawns — a bright blue shipping container stocked with footballs, cornhole sets, binoculars and scavenger-hunt kits, all lent out at no charge. It is open every weekend from 12 to 4pm through November 1. Whoever borrows the equipment leaves a valid ID with Visitor Services for the length of the rental. Getting there from Brooklyn is the easy part: the Trust for Governors Island runs summer weekend and holiday ferries from Brooklyn Bridge Park’s Pier 6 and from Red Hook’s Atlantic Basin, and those boats are free for everyone before 11am. Children 12 and under ride free at any hour, and adults otherwise pay $5 round trip. Schedules and tickets are at govisland.org . Lidl | Williamsburg | NOW OPEN The Williamsburg Lidl is open. The German discount grocer opened at 573 Grand Street on Wednesday, August 26, with a ribbon-cutting at 7:40am, and trades 8am to 9pm daily. It is the chain’s fifth Brooklyn store. Lidl’s pitch is price — mostly own-label stock, a bakery running all day, and a rotating aisle of non-food bargains on Mondays — which makes it worth knowing about if you are feeding a family in a corner of Williamsburg that has not had a full-size supermarket within easy reach. Bay Ridge Pilates | Bay Ridge | NOW OPEN Bay Ridge Pilates has opened at 8912 3rd Avenue, right on schedule for its planned August debut. The reformer and mat studio runs classes seven days a week — 7am to 8:30pm on weekdays, 8am to 2pm on weekends — with an introductory offer of three classes for $95 and a founding-member rate of $160 a month for unlimited classes for the first 100 clients. Cheese Plate | Williamsburg | NOW OPEN Cheese Plate has opened a third Brooklyn shop at 175 Kent Avenue, alongside the original Park Slope store and the Carroll Gardens branch. It is a specialty counter rather than a sit-down spot: artisanal cheese, charcuterie, tinned fish, confections, pastries and pantry staples, plus made-to-order boards that start around $125 and scale up to feed twenty — useful the next time you are volunteered for a class potluck or a birthday spread. The Williamsburg shop is open until 8pm. Rockaway Ocean Club | Riis Park | NOW OPEN The Rockaways’ landmark 1932 Riis Park bathhouse — a bridge away from Brooklyn and largely shuttered since the early 1970s — reopened in mid-August as Rockaway Ocean Club after an $88 million restoration. The restaurants (one Peruvian, one American), café, gelato and the events courtyard are open to all beachgoers; the pool and garden are members-only, with kids 16 and under welcome there until 7pm. The beach itself stays public and free. During the preview season the club runs Friday–Sunday. Coming soon to our neighborhoods Brooklyn Science Spot | Carroll Gardens | Opening in September A two-floor science hangout will open up at 478 Court Street, with a chemistry lab, a live animal room stocked with turtles, lizards and frogs, and a STEM toy shop upstairs. After-school classes are grouped by age from preschool up to ten, and registration is open for the fall session, which runs September through December on the NYC school calendar. Birthday parties, holiday camps and school trips too. BKCM Prospect Park West | Park Slope The Brooklyn Conservatory of Music is opening a second Park Slope location in September, four blocks from the brownstone on Seventh Avenue it has occupied since 1944. The 12,000-square-foot space holds 13 new studios, a 1,450-square-foot performance hall and a community gathering room, with its own entrance and an ADA-accessible ramp at 963 President Street, on Grand Army Plaza. Early childhood music classes and all on-site music therapy move here, into the new BKCM Music Therapy Institute. First day of classes and lessons is Monday, September 14, and fall registration is open at both locations. The Goddard School of Greenpoint | Greenpoint Brooklyn’s first Goddard School is fitting out 85 Commercial Street, with classrooms running from three-month-old infants through pre-K, an indoor gym, a literacy nook, a playground and hours of 7am to 6pm on weekdays. The school is booking tours and taking enrolment now; its own page still reads “coming soon,” and no opening date has been published. Carroll Gardens Library | Carroll Gardens | Reopening late 2026 tbc The landmarked Carnegie branch at 396 Clinton Street is close to the finish line, but still without a date. Brooklyn Public Library told Brooklyn Bridge Parents that the last major piece of the project is the elevator: once it is installed and inspected, which the library expects next month, the final work can begin — new furniture and shelving, setting up the technology and getting many thousands of books back on the shelves. A specific reopening date will follow that inspection. The two-part renovation adds a dedicated teen space on the mezzanine, the new elevator, new heating and cooling, new lighting and upgraded bathrooms. Until the branch is back, the pop-up library at 250 Baltic Street is open Monday and Tuesday 1–5pm, Thursday 3–7pm and Friday 10am–2pm. Red Hook Library | Red Hook | Reopening 2027 Closed since March 2023, the Wolcott Street branch is getting a $17.5 million rebuild that adds a dedicated children’s room for storytimes, a teen space, a community room and two reservable meeting rooms, along with 14 percent more public space and outdoor reading and activity areas. It is designed as a net zero building with rooftop solar. Brooklyn Public Library told Brooklyn Bridge Parents it now expects Red Hook to reopen in early 2027, later than the 2026 completion previously announced. The interim branch at 362 Van Brunt Street stays open in the meantime. The 2026–27 school calendar The first day of school for all 3K–12 New York City public school students is Thursday, September 10, 2026 — a late start, since Labor Day lands late this year. The last day is Monday, June 28, 2027. The full calendar is on the city’s website here . Charter and private schools start either Tuesday, September 8 or Wednesday, September 9 depending on the school. A few dates worth writing down now: Yom Kippur, Monday, September 21, 2026 is the first day off of the new year, less than two weeks after the first day of school. Italian Heritage / Indigenous Peoples’ Day, Monday, October 12, 2026 is a day off. Election Day, Tuesday, November 3, 2026 is a remote learning day for students this year, not a day off. Veterans Day, Wednesday, November 11, 2026 is a day off. Thanksgiving recess runs Thursday, November 26 and Friday, November 27, 2026. Winter recess runs Thursday, December 24, 2026 through Friday, January 1, 2027. Classes resume Monday, January 4. Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Monday, January 18, 2027 is a day off. No bonus days off from Rosh Hashanah, Diwali or Juneteenth this year — all three fall on weekends, and Lunar New Year lands inside the February midwinter recess. Students are scheduled for 177 days of class, plus two half days for parent-teacher conferences. Day Camps when public schools are closed Our school break camp guide profiles local camps across Brooklyn Heights, DUMBO, Downtown Brooklyn, Fort Greene, Boerum Hill, Cobble Hill, Carroll Gardens, Gowanus, Park Slope, Prospect Heights, Crown Heights, Bed-Stuy, Sunset Park,, Bay Ridge, Williamsburg, Greenpoint, and beyond. These camps are open when public schools are closed, offering programs for children ages 2 to 14. For each listing, we’ve included program details, age groups, hours, costs, and contact information. Immunizations and health forms Every student entering a New York school has to be up to date on required vaccines, and two deadlines catch families off guard every year: sixth graders who have turned 11 need a Tdap booster, and seventh graders need the meningococcal (MenACWY) vaccine. Check the state’s 2026–27 immunization requirements against your child’s records now, while pediatricians still have August appointments. School news Year two of the phone ban New York’s bell-to-bell restriction on smartphones, smartwatches and other personal internet-enabled devices stays in place for 2026–27. What is new: as of September 1, every district and charter school has to publish an annual report on how it enforced the policy last year, including non-identifying demographic data on students disciplined for non-compliance and, where the numbers show disparities, a plan to address them. Pre-K special education expands into Brooklyn classrooms For the first time, the city’s Nest, Horizon, AIMS, Path and ACES programs are coming to Pre-K, backed by a $67.5 million investment reaching 14 school districts this fall. Brooklyn sites include P.S. 147 Isaac Remsen in District 14; P.S. 015 Patrick Daly, P.S. 024 and P.S. 958 in District 15; the District 20 Pre-K Center; and P.S. 212 Lady Deborah Moody in District 21. The city is also hiring hundreds of staff to shorten evaluation wait times, expand bilingual assessments and help families through the IEP process. Full details are here .
