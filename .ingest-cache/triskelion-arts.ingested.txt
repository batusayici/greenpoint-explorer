A NOTE FROM THE CURATORS
The BLACK BLACK BLACK Week returns to Triskelion Arts September 23 - 25 for its fourth consecutive year!! Bringing together dancers, choreographers, musicians, visual artists, and multidisciplinary creators for a powerful three day celebration of Black artistry, culture, and community.
This year, we are coming back to each other! FAMILY REUNION is an invitation to gather, reconnect, and remember that we are part of something bigger than ourselves. Whether you arrive with your people, find your people in the room, or come through on your own, there is a place for you here. For BBB, family is not only about where we come from. It’s about what we choose to carry forward. It’s about honoring the people, practices, and possibilities that have made our communities what they are, while making room for what comes next.
Throughout the week, we’ll create space for movement, music, conversation, reflection, and joy. We’ll celebrate the artists & elders who have poured into this community, make room to rest and be cared for, and remind ourselves that gathering can be an act of healing in itself. This is a reunion in every sense! A return to one another, a return to ourselves, and a celebration of the Black dance community we continue to build together.
Guests are encouraged to arrive early to experience immersive community activations, share soul food plates, and connect through rituals that open up conversation, reflection, and belonging. Each evening begins with community breathwork accompanied by live drumming before moving into performances that honor the richness, resilience, and creativity of the Black experience.
Doors open at 6:30pm to experience :
Live Family Portraits by Dig Ferreira
Community Mural facilitated by Hakeem Olayinka
Rituals for Remembering by Mawu Gora & Carter
Reunion for Your Revolution with Marla Lou
Photobooth led by Geoffrey Baptiste
Hot Soul-food Plates by Elevate Cater NYC
Curated Cocktails Hous of Potions
At 7:30 we commence our performances
With guided breathwork by JOYBOY and drumming by Kala Brame .
Photo by Luis Suarez
BEYOND THE BLACK BOX
Beyond the Black Box (BBB) is an arts and cultural non-profit organization dedicated to amplifying Black voices, fostering connections, and educating through immersive experiences. Our mission is to celebrate, honor, and uplift the Black dance community by creating supportive spaces for artists to thrive. Motivated by a desire to address the lack of representation and support we encountered in our own journeys, we are committed to promoting inclusiveness, fairness, and visibility in the dance world.
BBB achieves its mission by producing dynamic multidisciplinary works, including live dance productions, community engagement initiatives, mentorship programs, and educational opportunities. Our approach seamlessly weaves together elements of popular culture with boundless enthusiasm, showcasing the remarkable accomplishments of our community.
With your support, BBB will continue to empower Black dancers and artists who champion social and political principles of inclusivity, fairness, and visibility, ultimately enriching the cultural tapestry of our community. Together, we will create a brighter future where Black voices are celebrated and heard through the transformative power of the arts.
@beyondtheblackbox

WE GATHER HERE
Somewhere between the water line and the wall, WE GATHER HERE... Same circle. Still becoming. Carriers of memory. Keepers of the count. The ones who never fully left, and the ones returning with outside eyes. Pulled home to the family reunion. Born at the crossroads of Octavia Butler's Parable of the Talents, Brother from Another Planet, and The Wiz, WE GATHER HERE is a dance-theater work and sound bath experience imagining the intimate spaces where we retreat to confide and rehabilitate.
COLLABORATORS
Performed by Christian Warner, DaMond Lemonte Garner
Set Design by Imani Gaudin

Photo by Spencer Weidie
CHRISTIAN A. WARNER
Christian A. Warner, a native of Houston, TX, is an interdisciplinary performance artist, choreographer, sound designer, and director working across movement, sound, and film. His choreography has been commissioned by Pepatian / BAAD, Triskelion Arts, JCAL, Performa/Dance, Ballet Co.Laboratory, and the BlackLight Summit. He is a 2023 Heinz Endowment Creative Development Awardee, a 2025 New York Dance Festival Choreographic Fellow, and a 2025 Princess Grace Award winner in Choreography.
He has danced with companies and artist such as Sidra Bell Dance New York, Boca Tuya, NVA & Guests, GauDanse, slowdanger, TU Dance, Owen/Cox Dance Group, Deeply Rooted Dance Theatre, Ponybox Dance Theatre, and Kayla Farrish. His sound design has been featured in GRAPH for Sidra Bell Dance New York at the Metropolitan Museum of Art's exhibit "Flight To Egypt" and Imani Gaudin's production nanibu, premiering at the Marigny Opera House in New Orleans, Louisiana.
@itschristianalexander

A Beast that Came Apart Mid-Air
(Pt. 1) Work In Process
A Beast that Came Apart Mid-Air is an Afrosurrealist dance-theater performance taking shape as a visual mixtape through movement, voice, narrative, film, folktale, and spiritual during a reckoning. Migrating portals in-and-out of time, 6 BIPOC characters traverse interconnected maps of resistance and uncover those who subverted power to exist. They transform historic resilience into learning how to wield power to reimagine the future. Dismantling the beast of disempowerment, what liberation and imagination lives? In an immersive dance-theater play, the audience moves through their inner worlds, forgotten pasts, and brave pathways of darkness and revolution for lives where we can be whole.
This work has been supported by NY Support for Artist 2026, Danse Mirage Foundation Inc. Choreography Commission 2026, Mercury Store Residency, Ellis Beauregard Foundation Residency, NYFA/NYSCA Choreography Fellowship, CUNY Dance Initiative- Brooklyn College (2025-2026) and Hunter College (2026-2027), Triskelion Arts!!, BAX AIR Residency 2026-2028, and The Pocantico Center Residency.
COLLABORATORS
Directed & Created by Kayla Farrish in co-creation & collaboration with artists: Jamal Abrams, Marirosa Crawford, Kennedy Hill, Kendall Ramirez, Kar’mel Small, Junyla Silmon, Tashae Udo

KAYLA FARRISH
Kayla Farrish is a Black American Director merging dance-theater, filmmaking, narrative, and sound score. Her commissions include Limon Dance Company, Whim W’him, Ririe Woodbury, Armstrong Now!, Danspace, Little Island, Harlem Stage, American Dance Festival and beyond. She creates live works, films, site-specific/immersive pieces, and collaborations with musicians such as Esperanza Spalding, Trixie Whitley, Moor Mother, and Melanie Charles. Presenting spaces include Lincoln Center, Park Avenue Armory, Symphony Space, and National Sawdust, Joyce Theater, among receiving support from Works & Process, Brooklyn Filmmakers Collective, Baryshnikov Arts, La Mama Theater, and others.
She received the Sundance Uprise Grant for Emerging BIPOC Directors, Bessie Awards for December 8th and NYLA’s “Motherboard Suite”, New York Times Top 2021 Dance Performances “Roster” and “Breakout Star.” She is a recipient of the Harkness Promise Award for 2022. She premiered reimagined archival work for Limon at Joyce Theater (2024). She toured “Put Away the Fire, dear” supported by NEFA National Dance Project. She received Watermill Center’s Baroness Nina Von Maltzahn Fellowship and the Ellis-Beauregard Foundation Choreographer Award (2023). She is a 2025 CUNY Dance Initiative Artist, NYFA/NYSCA Choreography Fellow, 2026 NY Support for Artists Grantee, and Danse Mirage Foundation Choreography commissioned artist.
@fulloutfarrish

Warm Greetings from one long day in the desert
Warm Greetings from one long day in the desert is an evening-length multidisciplinary dance and performance duet by Japanese-American artist Kimiko Tanabe and Moroccan American artist Nora Alami. Through installation, projection, and experimental movement, the performance transforms the theater into a surreal desert landscape, shifting from the terrifyingly solitary to the magically hospitable. Warm Greetings from one long day in the desert blends dance and visual design inviting audiences to reconsider the “Orient” within contemporary American imagination.
COLLABORATORS

ABOUT THE ARTISTS
Kimiko Tanabe is a fourth generation Japanese-American experimental dance artist moving through the social, cultural, and political landscape of post-internment Japanese-America. She is currently the 2025-2026 Trisk Fellow along with Nora Alami at Triskelion Arts and 2026 Chocolate Factory Artist in Residence.
@kimiko_tanabe
Nora Alami is a Moroccan American experimental dance artist. Dance is her tool for conducting research, building community, and organizing politically. Her artistry experiments with desire and disparate realities through embodiment, writing, performance, and materiality. She is the 2025-2026 Trisk Fellow with Kimiko Tanabe at Triskelion Arts and a PhD candidate in Performance Studies.
@noralami

MIZUHO KAPPA
A Dream of Larva
A Dream of Larva is a hybrid dance-theater work integrating movement, text, and visual images to explore psychological survival within domestic violence. Through the work, I investigate resilience in the face of violence, asking how people continue to survive, adapt, and find connection despite repeated cycles of personal and collective trauma. Rather than depicting violence itself, the work explores the body's capacity to endure, transform, and imagine new possibilities for living.
Photo by Ava Pellor
Mizuho Kappa is a choreographer and performing artist originally from Osaka, Japan, with a degree in Entomology.
She has recently performed with Punchdrunk’s Sleep No More NY, Peridance Contemporary Dance Company, Yoshiko Chuma & The School of Hard Knocks, Ballet Collective, among others.
Her self-choreographed solo works have been presented at The Shed, La MaMa, Gene Frankel Theater, TheaterLab, The Brick, Ars Nova, NOoSPHERE ARTS and more.
She frequently collaborates with cross-disciplinary artists, including a neuroscience researcher, transmedia playwright, hybrid writer and puppet designer. These collaborative works have been showcased at the New Museum, New York Hall of Science, Open Source Gallery, Grace Exhibition Space, City University of Hong Kong, House of Yes, the Chocolate Factory and beyond.
Kappa is a commissioned choreographer for Peridance Contemporary Dance Company ‘25 Season, and a recipient of the GALLIM Moving Artist Residency '25, Mark Morris SharedSpace Residency '26.
Her choreographic voice is recognized for conveying narrative through raw expression, unexpected physicality, and striking surrealistic tableaux, described as “evoking and unhinged” by Brooklyn Magazine and “potent” by The New York Times.
@mizup0i_kappa/
COLLABORATORS
Performers: Andrea Farley Shimota, Mizuho Kappa
Lighting design by Connor Sale
DYLAN RICHMOND
There’s an eclipse happening all the time
It is no wonder clouds darken before they rain/ they are starting to feel more like us.
Imagine this work as a solo. Imagine a duet. What might be gained to imagine it as both? What must be sacrificed? This piece investigates an embodied reality of W.E.B Du Bois’ writings on “double consciousness.” Through poetry, projections, and highly-physical movement, Richmond researches the maddening pervasiveness of hyper-surveillance, reckoning with America’s perpetual problematization of Blackness.
Photo by Amira Oguntoyinbo.
Dylan Richmond is a choreographer, dancer, researcher, and poet from the Connecticut shoreline. Dylan has presented work at TOTAH, WestFest, NYU, The Tank, Bowdoin College; and lectured at Yale University, UCLA, and Connecticut College. As a poet, Dylan is a Pushcart Prize nominee and is notably published in A Gathering of the Tribes, From Root to Seed: Black, Brown, and Indigenous Poets Write the Northeast, Sibling Rivalry Press, Lily Poetry Review, and No, Dear . He also shares with his twin the 2020 “Excellence in Journalism Award” from the Connecticut Society of Professional Journalism. Dylan is the Company Manager and Live Gallery Producer for New York Live Arts and the Bill T. Jones/Arnie Zane Company. He graduated cum laude from Bowdoin College with a B.A. in Dance and English where he received the President’s Award.
@choreopoet
