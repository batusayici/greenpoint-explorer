## Williamsburg Is Getting Two 100% Affordable Housing Buildings
https://greenpointers.com/2026/08/07/williamsburg-is-getting-two-100-affordable-housing-buildings/
Fri, 07 Aug 2026 13:00:00 +0000
Luxury towers and marinas are not the only developments coming to North Brooklyn. Affordable housing is also on the way.
The Slate Property Group and the RiseBoro Community Partnership recently announced that they had secured financing to build two 100% affordable housing buildings in Williamsburg, at 178 Montrose Avenue. and 73 Meserole Street.
The project consists of one 14-story and one 13-story building that will deliver 312 units – 163 in one building and 149 in the other. 60% of the units will be reserved for formerly homeless people. The rest will be affordable to low-income households, the developers said.
“As one of the city’s most expensive neighborhoods, Williamsburg has been out of reach for far too many working families,” said David Schwartz, co-founder and principal of Slate Property Group, during the announcement covered by the New York Real Estate Journal . “This project aims to fix that.”
The development will include a mix of unit sizes, from studios to three-bedroom apartments, with rents starting at $1,339 a month for studios to $2,304 for three-bedroom apartments.
Designed by Aufgang Architects, the all-electric buildings will offer social services on site, as well as amenities including outdoor and indoor recreation areas, fitness facilities and laundry. RiseBoro is to provide full-time security and maintenance staff.
The project adds to a growing supply of affordable housing announced for Greenpoint and Williamsburg over the last month. Recently announced developments include projects on Oak Street and the Broadway Triangle as well as the affordable housing commitment for Monitor Point project . Altogether, these developments are expected to bring more than 2,000 affordable units to the area.

## Checkmate: North Brooklyn Chess Club Sets Up Summer Residency at the McCarren Parkhouse
https://greenpointers.com/2026/08/06/checkmate-north-brooklyn-chess-club-sets-up-summer-residency-at-the-mccarren-parkhouse/
Thu, 06 Aug 2026 17:17:54 +0000
Born out of the pandemic, the North Brooklyn Chess Club got its start as a pre-party fixture at the Greenpoint bar Ponyboy , where neighbors gathered around chessboards before the evening crowds.
This summer, the chess club has found a new home through its residency at the McCarren Parkhouse (855 Lorimer St.) hosting weekly chess nights every Thursday from 7 to 11pm. Whether you are learning how the pieces move or you are a seasoned competitor, the chess club welcomes you. Events offer game reviews and guidance from experienced chess masters while the club’s “Challenger’s Corner” gives advanced players the space to test their skills against top competition.
Photo: North Brooklyn Chess Club
Heather Brown, president of the North Brooklyn Chess Club, says she’s always looking for creative partnerships that make chess more accessible while bolstering connections across the neighborhood.
“A workout class that incorporates chess, like holding a plank while playing a chess game,” Brown told Greenpointers , is one example of the kind of cross-community pollination that she would like to see more of this summer. Brown hopes to introduce newbies to the game while continuing to build on Greenpoint’s pre-existing chess community.
For those looking to see what the club is all about, head to the Parkhouse on August 13 to experience the club’s signature “Challenger’s Corner.” Tickets can be purchased here .

## Greenpoint at Work: Radamés Millán of San Germán Records
https://greenpointers.com/2026/08/06/greenpoint-at-work-radames-millan-of-san-german-records/
Thu, 06 Aug 2026 13:00:00 +0000
Our “Greenpoint at Work” interview series takes a look at the lives of people living and/or working in the Greenpoint-Williamsburg area.
We recently spoke to Radamés Millán, who has been running San Germán Records at 89 Moore Street in Williamsburg since the 1970s, offering Puerto Rican souvenirs, clothes, food and music.
Read our most recent “Greenpoint at Work” profile here .
This interview has been edited and condensed for clarity.
Tell us about yourself and how you founded San Germán Records.
I’m Puerto Rican, born and raised in the town of San Germán, a small but culturally rich community in the southwest of the island. From a young age, I enjoyed being involved in my community. I often organized benefit events to support young people in my hometown. Those experiences taught me the value of bringing people together.
About 70 years ago, I moved to New York and settled on Long Island. I got my first job working in a restaurant. At the time, my English was very limited, and to be honest, I wasn’t very enthusiastic about learning the language. Still, I worked my way up. I stayed at that restaurant for more than 10 years.
Eventually I felt ready to become a manager, but the opportunity never came. That’s when I decided to start my own business. I began selling LP records door to door on Long Island. Later, a friend suggested I move my business to Brooklyn. I took his advice, and that’s how I ended up here, and it’s been fantastic.
San Germán Records was originally a record store.
Yes, we’ve done so many great things over the years that I would never have dreamed of. I founded a record label, where we recorded a great deal of traditional Puerto Rican music. Promoting and preserving that music has always been very important to me. The albums we produced, along with records from many other labels, were what we sold in the store. At that time, Williamsburg had a large Puerto Rican population, so Puerto Rican music was by far the most popular. Around 80% of the people living in this area were Puerto Rican.
In the 1970s and 1980s, reggaeton didn’t exist yet, or if it did, it was still very underground. Salsa became more popular later. The music people wanted to listen to was traditional Puerto Rican music, mainly because many of the families living here had come from rural parts of the island, where those musical traditions were deeply rooted.
Traditional Puerto Rican music such as jíbara, bomba or plena features instruments like the cuatro, guitars, maracas and bongos. Boleros were also very popular, especially recordings by trios like Los Panchos and Los Condes. That was the soundtrack of the community during those years.
Back then, the Puerto Rican community was vibrant and highly visible . Unfortunately, gentrification changed the neighborhood. Many families who had bought houses here at affordable prices eventually received attractive offers and decided to sell and left. Most of them have moved to Florida. A few of us have stayed. I decided not to throw in the towel, and I’m still here.
Photo: Greenpointers
In what ways have you continued to organize community events over the decades?
One of the most important events we organize is the annual Three Kings Parade. We created it to celebrate and share the biblical tradition of the Three Kings, which is observed every January 6 and is deeply rooted in Puerto Rican culture. I experienced that tradition firsthand as a child, and I’ve always been committed to keeping it alive here in Brooklyn.
In 2027, we’ll celebrate the parade’s 30th anniversary. The event will take place on January 10 along Graham Avenue, and we’re planning something very special to mark this milestone. The parade is a day filled with live music, dancers, colorful floats and vibrant costumes. Over the years, we’ve welcomed many headline performers. Some of the biggest names in salsa have participated, along with artists such as Daddy Yankee, La India, Tito Rojas and Jerry Rivera, just to name a few.
Given that San Germán Records has evolved over the years, what sets it apart today?
Today my store offers all kinds of products that celebrate Puerto Rican culture and traditions. Visitors can find traditional candies and foods, including Puerto Rican cake and arroz con dulce along with a wide variety of souvenirs.
Of course, we have the coquí in every form imaginable. It would be unthinkable not to. The coquí is a tiny frog native to Puerto Rico, famous for the distinctive sound it makes at night, and it has become one of the island’s most recognizable symbols. We also carry countless items featuring the Puerto Rican flag.
Another popular item is the pava, the traditional straw hat worn by Puerto Rican farmers to protect themselves from the intense Caribbean sun, especially during the years when sugar cane was one of the island’s main crops. More recently, the pava has gained new visibility thanks to Bad Bunny.
Sports memorabilia is also an important part of what we offer. Puerto Rico has produced legendary athletes, particularly in boxing and baseball. Figures like Félix “Tito” Trinidad and Roberto Clemente have become icons far beyond the island. Visitors can find jerseys and memorabilia honoring these and many other sports legends.
For Puerto Ricans, these items are more than souvenirs. They reconnect us with our memories, our heritage and our identity. Boricuas are very proud of where we come from, so anything that represents the island is sacred for us.
Photo: Greenpointers
What are your plans for continuing to promote community events?
Before talking about plans, I’d like to mention something important. As Williamsburg has undergone gentrification, some of the newer residents have tried to erase parts of the neighborhood’s Puerto Rican history. In 2023, the city Department of Transportation removed the street signs identifying Graham Avenue as “Avenue of Puerto Rico,” even though that honorary designation had been approved decades earlier by the New York City Council. For our community, those signs recognize the generations of Puerto Ricans who lived here and whose contributions helped shape Williamsburg’s identity.
I was proud to be part of the effort to have those signs reinstated. Many Puerto Ricans believe their removal reflected real estate interests. We think that perhaps some developers or property owners would rather not tell prospective tenants they’re moving to a neighborhood whose main street is known as “Avenue of Puerto Rico” because that would create fear among certain clients.
One of the ways we responded was by bringing the National Puerto Rican Day Parade back to Williamsburg in 2023. Although it was held for only one year, it was incredibly meaningful for the community. My hope is to make that parade a permanent tradition once again, specifically on Graham Avenue.
There is already a Puerto Rican Day Parade on Knickerbocker Avenue in Bushwick, but in my opinion, Graham Avenue is the natural home for this celebration . It’s the only street officially recognized as the Avenue of Puerto Rico, and it’s where the original parade was held. Unfortunately, we couldn’t continue after 2023 because of budget constraints. But if we can secure the necessary support and sponsorship, I’d love to bring it back. If individuals, organizations or businesses are interested in helping make that happen, my door is always open.
What’s coming up in the next few months for San Germán Records?
In September, we host a block party right outside the store. It’s a celebration of Puerto Rican culture with live music and entertainment throughout the day. We set up a stage on the street, and the festivities run from 12 to 6 pm. This year, the block party will take place on September 12, and everyone’s welcome.
