## New Coffee Shop Loco Cafe to Open in Former Home of Viva La Pizza
https://greenpointers.com/2026/08/04/new-coffee-shop-loco-cafe-to-open-in-former-home-of-viva-la-pizza/
Tue, 04 Aug 2026 17:00:00 +0000
A new coffee shop is taking over the 500 square-foot space that was previously home to Viva La Pizza and Pizza Prince . Loco Cafe is aiming to open next week, but they told Greenpointers they do not have an exact date yet, but it “should be around the second week of August.”
Loco Cafe is the third eatery to call the storefront home in two years. After Pizza Prince closed in 2024, Viva La Pizza took its place for only a year and then closed.
Viva La Pizza’s Jose Colohua said that the space was not financially sustainable, echoing the same reason for Pizza Prince’s closure. The storefront was previously listed for a monthly rent of $15,000, though it’s unclear what Viva La Pizza actually paid.
Real estate agent Eric Lupo told Greenpointers that before Loco Cafe took over, the rent was listed $10,000 a month. He said that Loco Cafe signed a lease for $8,500 a month “with terms.”
While Pizza Prince and Viva La Pizza used the address of 86 Nassau Avenue, Loco Cafe is using the address 626 Manhattan Avenue. Lupo explained that the building contains the addresses 626 Manhattan Avenue and 82-86 Nassau Avenue, “since it’s a corner building.” Lupo confirmed that Loco Cafe rented the former pizzeria and that the deli located in the corner of the building remains open.
The exterior of Viva La Pizza, which opened in the former home of Pizza Prince in Greenpoint. Photo: Julia Moak
Loco Cafe told Greenpointers that the neighborhood’s newest coffee shop will be a specialty coffee shop focused on serving high-quality coffee in a “welcoming, community-driven space.”
“Our goal is to create a neighborhood café where people can enjoy carefully crafted espresso drinks, quality pastries, and simple, fresh breakfast options,” Loco Cafe said. “We’re putting a strong emphasis on hospitality, consistency, and making everyone who walks through the door feel at home.”
Loco Cafe called its space “cozy” and “Instagram-worthy.” They said that every detail is designed to create an “experience,” in addition to serving coffee.
“Whether you’re meeting friends, working, or just stopping by for your daily coffee, we want Loco Cafe to be your go-to spot,” the newcomer said.

## Small Business Owners in Greenpoint and Williamsburg Welcome New City Reforms
https://greenpointers.com/2026/08/04/small-business-owners-in-greenpoint-and-williamsburg-welcome-new-city-reforms/
Tue, 04 Aug 2026 15:33:52 +0000
Small business owners in Greenpoint and Williamsburg are welcoming “ OPEN for Small Business ,” a package of reforms Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced on July 20. The reforms look to reduce paperwork, cut bureaucracy, speed up business openings and clarify compliance requirements for the city’s roughly 180,000 small businesses.
The package includes proposals to eliminate the separate permit restaurants need to serve ice cream; waive, for one year, the Department of Environmental Protection’s $110 registration fee for cooking equipment and food trucks and expand the child-care permit portal to include renewals, modifications and guidance on review processes conducted by the Department of Buildings and the Fire Department.
“It’s great to see this program launch!” Perri Salka, owner and curator of the gift shop The WonderMart (141 India St.), told Greenpointers.
Salka also volunteers as the lead organizer of Shop Small Greenpoint, an initiative run by local shop owners that organizes a twice-a-year retail crawl to promote independently owned brick-and-mortar shops, increase their visibility and cultivate community.
“From what I can see, these reforms do address many of the main challenges that small businesses face,” she said, “and I am sure the program will only continue to evolve.”
“OPEN for Small Business” also includes an executive order expanding the mission of the Department of Small Business Services’ NYC BEST program. Under the order, the city will assign a dedicated client manager to everyone seeking to open a business. According to the proposed list of reforms , these managers “will stay with them from start to finish, offering individual case management, compliance education before an inspection so owners know what will be checked, and support after a summons is issued.”
The reforms also expand access to street vending by eliminating the Health Department’s requirement that owners pay any outstanding monetary penalties to receive an initial food vendor permit. “Right now the process is very difficult because permits take too long, cost too much and require too many steps,” Jaime Cancino, owner of El Tío Tamale Cart outside the Nassau Avenue station on the G train, told Greenpointers.
Although Cancino managed to navigate the process and opened his cart a little more than a year ago, he said many of his friends and family members have repeatedly struggled with the bureaucracy involved in starting a business.
“I think it’s a very positive initiative, and I hope these improvements are implemented in the short term,” Cancino said.
El Tío Tamale Cart. Photo: Greenpointers
During the announcement, Julie Su, deputy mayor for economic justice, said “opening a business in the city can feel like The Odyssey.” For many small business owners in the neighborhood, the endless challenges include another monster to overcome: the G train’s repeated weekend shutdowns .
A recent survey led by Shop Small Greenpoint on the impact of MTA service changes along the G line from July 2025 to July 2026 found that 91% of businesses reported a decrease in sales during shutdown weekends, with sales falling 20% to 24% on average.
The shutdowns began in 2024 as part of the MTA’s four-year signal modernization project, expected to be completed in 2027.
The struggles to stay afloat felt particularly severe in 2025, several restaurant owners said . That same year, the NYC Five Chamber Alliance, a coalition of the boroughs’ Chambers of Commerce, reported a deepening economic crisis for small businesses . Although 4,660 new businesses opened, an estimated 5,700 closed, resulting in a net loss of 20,000 jobs in the sector, according to the Alliance.
For small business owners in Williamsburg, a decline in foot traffic is among their top concerns , a recent survey by the Department of Small Business Services in partnership with the Grand Street and Graham Avenue Business Improvement Districts (BIDs) found.
The survey also showed that personal-care businesses, including beauty salons, barbershops and spas, made up the largest business category in Williamsburg, with more than 100. For those businesses, the reform package proposes streamlining licensing requirements, extending barber shop permit renewals from an annual to a three-year cycle, and reducing overlapping regulatory requirements.
Currently, a barber shop owner needs three separate permits to operate: a state barber occupational license to cut hair, a state Barber Shop Owner/Renter License to run the shop and a city Barber Shop Permit. The city and state have committed to working together to simplify these requirements, according to the proposed reforms.
While many of the changes may take time to implement, small business owners say they welcome the city’s acknowledgment of longstanding obstacles and its commitment to addressing them. “I think it will make business owners feel like they have a true partner in the city helping them,” said Salka. “Before, they always had to navigate city agencies on their own with little guidance.”
