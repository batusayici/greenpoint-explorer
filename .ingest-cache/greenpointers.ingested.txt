## New New Patch, Founded by a Greenpoint Mom, Saves Clothes One Sticker at a Time
https://greenpointers.com/2026/08/11/new-new-patch-founded-by-a-greenpoint-mom-saves-clothes-one-sticker-at-a-time/
Tue, 11 Aug 2026 13:00:00 +0000
“It all started with a blueberry,” Prue Linehan told Greenpointers .
Linehan, a Greenpoint local and mom of two, founded a company right here in the neighborhood. It’s called New New Patch and sells Sticker Patches that save kids’ clothes from things like blueberry stains.
The idea for New New Patch originated when Linehan’s four-year-old son was an infant. He loved food, but destroyed his clothes. The new mom didn’t have the time or energy for stain removal or sewing, so she would throw the clothes away because it was easier and often cheaper to buy new ones.
New New Patch’s Sticker Patches. Photo: Katt Jones
“When I looked for a quick fix, everything I found required an iron, sewing skills or came in the form of thick embroidered patches. None of it felt designed for how modern families actually live,” Linehan explained. Her search for an easy option began. Specifically, she wanted a peel and stick fabric patch that stuck to cotton kids’ clothes and lasted in the wash.
Linehan cold-called adhesive manufacturers asking for samples, turned her living room into a research and development lab, and spent countless hours testing products. After 45 different adhesives and 1,000 test cycles later, she found one that was washable and flexible enough to become New New Patch’s signature item, Sticker Patches.
New New Patch’s owner Prue Linehan at a pop-up at Giggles and Wiggles. Photo: New New Patch
After 15 years of building brands and leading marketing operations for companies like Colossal Media, Etsy and Cash App, New New Patch is Linehan’s full time job. She has a manufacturing partner and access to a team of product designers for future product ideas. Linehan doesn’t have a background in material science or product engineering, but simply became “obsessed” with solving a problem that many new moms face.
Linehan told Greenpointers that she didn’t do it alone. “Sticker Patches may have been invented by me, but my Greenpoint village brought it to life,” she explained. New New Patch’s illustrator, brand designer, photographer, product testers, and first child model are all in Greenpoint.
Even the brand’s first retailer, resale shop Parachute (151 Norman Ave.), is local. Linehan said that the store’s team supported her throughout the three years of development and were the first to stock Sticker Patches once they launched.
“People often say it takes a village to raise a child. I think it takes one to build a business, too,” Linehan mused. “New New Patch is full of fingerprints from people who believed in the idea long before anyone else did.”
New New Patch’s Sticker Patches. Photo: Katt Jones
The Greenpoint-built New New Patch is about making something you already have feel new again. It can turn a stained piece into something your child wants to wear all over again.
“So it’s not new. It’s New New,” Linehan explained. “Our mission is to help families keep what they have in wear and make that choice as easy as buying new.”
New New Patch plans to expand beyond Sticker Patches through collaborations with artists and brands while exploring ways repair can become integrated into products from the start.
“Imagine buying a kids’ T-shirt that comes with a matching Sticker Patch sheet, so when the first stain inevitably happens, you already have everything you need,” Linehan said.

## Greenpoint Elected Officials Ask NYC Parks to Reconsider Controversial Transmitter Park Proposal
https://greenpointers.com/2026/08/10/greenpoint-elected-officials-ask-nyc-parks-to-reconsider-controversial-transmitter-park-proposal/
Mon, 10 Aug 2026 16:44:07 +0000
Greenpoint’s elected officials are speaking out against plans to construct a restaurant, marina, and public restroom at Transmitter Park , ahead of a public hearing scheduled for today, August 10.
City Council Member Lincoln Restler, State Senator Kristen Gonzalez, and State Assemblymember Emily Gallagher asked the NYC Parks Department to reconsider the controversial proposal, which sparked widespread backlash when Greenpointers first revealed the plans in July.
“Given our community’s nearly unanimous and vociferous opposition, we respectfully request that Parks pull back this proposal and work with our offices and community to identify a better path forward,” the letter reads. “We do not believe the maritime concession is appropriate for this park and would suggest a smaller footprint for a concession in the park — or no concession at all.”
WNYC Transmitter Park Letter – Aug 10 2026 Download
During an info session on August 6, several community members asserted that the larger-scale project was not a worthy trade-off for a public restroom, fearing it was a Trojan Horse that could usher in larger crowds and disturb the park’s peaceful waterfront vibe.
Two restaurant industry veterans, Josh Cohen and David Schmitt, were poised to sign a 25-year license agreement that allows for the development, operation, and maintenance of a restaurant and marina in the park. A preliminary draft revealed that plans could entail the park’s current building and erecting a new two-story building in its place.
The arrangement is through the Parks’ concessions program, which allows food service professionals to operate restaurants and cafes on Parks’ properties. In return, that revenue stream frees up Parks to finally build a public restroom in the park, a long-overdue goal for many in the neighborhood.
And if you’re wondering, “Well, why can’t Parks just decide to build a bathroom as is?” The answer is that building public restrooms in New York City is a bit more complicated (and expensive) than it may appear.
A public hearing will take place today at 2:30 p.m. (255 Greenwich St., 8th Floor, Manhattan). You can also email written testimony to fcrc@mocs.nyc.gov .
