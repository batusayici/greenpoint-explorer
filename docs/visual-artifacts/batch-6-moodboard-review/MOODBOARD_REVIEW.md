## Top 5 Strongest References

### 1. OCCII Community Drinks flyer

Why it is strong:
- It carries the emotional target most directly: community, invitation, playful confidence, and late-90s/early-2000s sim energy.
- The chunky white/navy typography and green attention markers are memorable without needing explanation.

Best use:
- Use as emotional/UI energy, not as a layout template.
- Translate its confidence into labels, markers, and place-card hierarchy.

### 2. RollerCoaster Tycoon Classic

Why it is strong:
- It shows how a small isometric scene can feel alive through tiny ambient details and inspection rewards.
- It supports the MVP loop without requiring avatar, NPC, or management systems.

Best use:
- Borrow dense-but-readable scene staging and small ambient life.

### 3. Peter Pan Donuts

Why it is strong:
- It is a real Greenpoint anchor and prevents the project from becoming generic.
- Its storefront identity can carry a lot of local specificity in one scene.

Best use:
- Treat storefront sign bands and facade rhythm as the core world layer.

### 4. Secret Riso Club - Bushwick Signs

Why it is strong:
- It reframes DIY energy as local sign/artifact culture rather than generic sticker decoration.
- It suggests how real neighborhood ephemera can inform UI and surface treatment.

Best use:
- Borrow selective print texture, sign-artifact thinking, and tactile imperfection.

### 5. eBoy Pixoramas

Why it is strong:
- It proves that dense urban pixel worlds can reward close looking.
- It supports the project's discovery emotion: the pleasure of noticing small details.

Best use:
- Borrow micro-detail density and poster-like collectability, but at a much smaller and calmer MVP scale.

## Top 5 References To Reject Or Use Cautiously

### 1. Isometric NYC

Use cautiously:
- Strong process reference, weak style north star for this MVP.
- Its scale, AI-generation challenge, and tiling concerns are not the product goal.

Avoid:
- Letting scale or map coverage become the achievement.

### 2. Civic/NPS map symbols

Use cautiously:
- Excellent for legibility discipline.
- Emotionally too institutional if it becomes the dominant UI language.

Avoid:
- Making the prototype feel like a public-services map.

### 3. Townscaper

Use cautiously:
- Useful for charm and massing.
- Too soft/toylike if followed closely.

Avoid:
- Losing storefront grit, signage, and Greenpoint specificity.

### 4. Dorfromantik

Use cautiously:
- Good reminder to preserve breathing room.
- Rural/cozy tone does not match Greenpoint's street texture.

Avoid:
- Muted softness that dulls urban discovery.

### 5. Habbo

Use cautiously:
- Useful for social-object readability and OCCII-adjacent interior energy.
- Risky because it implies avatars, rooms, and social systems that are out of scope.

Avoid:
- Anything that makes users expect walk mode, NPCs, or character interactions.

## What OCCII Specifically Contributes

OCCII contributes:

- Emotional temperature: open, social, unpretentious, event-like.
- Graphic confidence: large type, strong outlines, high-contrast hierarchy.
- Sim nostalgia: isometric interior, chunky furniture, low-poly/pixel-era game feel.
- Social cue language: green overhead markers that imply attention and presence.
- Product warning: the UI can easily overwhelm the explorable world if copied literally.

Translation for this project:

- Keep the confidence and social warmth.
- Use the thick white/navy treatment as an influence, not a clone.
- Let attention markers become abstract place/hotspot signals, not character plumbobs.
- Do not let a huge event-poster headline cover the map. The MVP needs the world to be clickable first.

## Repeating Visual Patterns Across The Board

- Fixed isometric viewpoint is consistently useful.
- Signage and labels are the fastest path to local specificity.
- Strong outlines improve readability and nostalgic game feel.
- Small ambient details make a static scene feel alive.
- UI needs a distinct layer, but it should not swallow the world.
- Limited palettes with one or two loud accents feel stronger than full-spectrum chaos.
- Local texture works best when grounded in real facade/sign/street details.
- Print/sticker language is strongest as overlay, card, marker, or surface accent.

## Key Tensions

### World vs UI

OCCII and DIY references push the UI to be loud. Greenpoint storefront references require the world to stay dominant.

Working implication:
- World detail should lead; UI should punctuate.

### Sim/game nostalgia vs product honesty

SimCity, RCT, Habbo, and OCCII create energy, but they can imply systems that are out of scope.

Working implication:
- Borrow screenshot language and readable affordances, not game mechanics.

### Print texture vs click clarity

Riso/sticker references create distinctiveness, but too much noise weakens hotspots and place recognition.

Working implication:
- Use texture selectively around cards, labels, posters, and sign edges.

### Charm vs Greenpoint grit

Townscaper/Dorfromantik charm can make the scene attractive, but Greenpoint needs lived-in storefront irregularity.

Working implication:
- Use charm in proportions and composition; use grit in signage, sidewalk clutter, and materials.

### Map clarity vs toy/diorama quality

NPS/map references bring structure, but too much clarity becomes utilitarian.

Working implication:
- Use map discipline under the hood, but present the scene as a playful miniature.

## What The Board Suggests

### World Detail Level

Recommended:
- Medium-high street-level detail.
- Fewer buildings, more specificity per building.
- Concentrate detail at storefront height: signs, awnings, windows, doors, trash bags, bike racks, subway entrance, tree pits.

Avoid:
- eBoy-level maximalism.
- Townscaper-level simplicity.

### UI Loudness

Recommended:
- Medium loudness.
- UI should feel sticker/flyer-informed but not headline-dominant.
- A marker or label can pop; the whole interface should not shout at once.

Avoid:
- OCCII-scale title dominance.
- NPS-level neutrality.

### Color Saturation

Recommended:
- Moderately saturated UI accents over warmer, grounded world colors.
- World palette: brick, cream, asphalt blue-gray, muted green, bakery yellow.
- UI accents: navy/white, acid green, one red or blue.

Avoid:
- Full riso rainbow.
- Beige-only restraint.
- Pure OCCII navy/white/green replication.

### Line Weight

Recommended:
- Strong outline language.
- Medium-heavy world silhouettes.
- Heavier UI/card outlines.
- Thin detail lines only for texture and windows.

Avoid:
- Clean vector thinness.
- Noisy pixel crust.

### Texture / Noise

Recommended:
- Selective texture.
- Brick rows, awning stripes, sign wear, sidewalk marks, mild print texture on UI/card elements.

Avoid:
- Applying riso/dither/noise everywhere.
- Textures that reduce click-target clarity.

### Signage Style

Recommended:
- Sign bands should be the main local identifier.
- Real place names only on correct businesses.
- Use local sign rhythms and storefront proportions as visual language.

Avoid:
- Fake event posters as core content.
- Generic coffee-shop signs.
- Exact brand reproduction that feels like endorsement.

### Charm vs Realism

Recommended:
- Pixel-inspired HD charm with truthful local cues.
- Slight compression and chunky proportions are fine if addresses and street logic stay honest.

Avoid:
- Documentary realism.
- Toy-town cuteness.

### Map Clarity vs Toy/Diorama Quality

Recommended:
- Build with map-like clarity but present as a handheld diorama.
- Use labels and markers as discoverable affordances, not utility-map pins.

Avoid:
- Municipal wayfinding look.
- Poster-only illustrated map.
