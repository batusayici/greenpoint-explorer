## What Was Decomposed

The source storefront was decomposed into the reusable visual layers most likely to determine whether the direction can scale:

- Facade shell: brick massing, roof cap, side-wall treatment, trim, pipes, vents, and depth cues.
- Sign band: cream or painted panel, trim rails, abstract marks, weathering, and small symbolic rhythm.
- Awning: fabric color, stripe pattern, scalloped edge, side hardware, and printed abstract symbol.
- Door/window pattern: narrow door, broad window, split-bay rhythm, mullion style, decals, and interior glow.
- Prop clusters: plants, crates, bench, sidewalk sign, utility box, bicycle-like street clutter, lamps, bins, and small fixtures.
- Poster/sticker treatment: torn paper patches, repeated fictional marks, edge wear, layering density, and color families.
- Fictional identity glyph treatment: non-word icons, partial marks, abstract symbol families, awning marks, and sign-panel motifs.
- Selected-state outline: clickable-region contour that follows storefront mass rather than a generic rectangle.
- Marker/card attachment zone: reusable anchor points, connector line, and paper-card content blocks.

The decomposition board intentionally keeps these pieces as rendered art fragments rather than a pure engineering diagram, because the decision depends on whether decomposition preserves atmosphere.

## What Was Reused

The recombination proof reuses the same underlying visual DNA:

- Brick-and-teal facade construction.
- Cream sign-band structure with hand-inked weathering.
- Mustard, dark, and striped awning family.
- Door/window modules with related trim and mullion language.
- Repeated prop families: planters, crates, lamps, benches, sidewalk signs, utility boxes, and poster sheets.
- Abstract glyph grammar based on diamonds, circles, leaf-like forms, chevrons, bars, and partial non-word marks.
- Worn paper UI card language, ink-dot connector, marker ring, and selected outline.

The reuse is meant to be visible enough to prove a system, but not so visible that variants collapse into clones.

## What Was Varied

Each recombined storefront changes decision-relevant traits rather than just color:

- Identity: each storefront uses a different abstract glyph family and sign/awning motif.
- Opening rhythm: variants shift between narrow corner kiosk, wide two-bay shop, workshop/cafe-like frontage, recessed bay, and compact row frontage.
- Props: each variant changes prop density, plant placement, poster grouping, crate/bench/sidewalk-sign use, and utility clutter.
- Signage treatment: sign bands vary in panel shape, hanging sign presence, awning symbol placement, color relationship, and mark scale.
- Attachment behavior: selected outlines and card anchors are shown on multiple storefront silhouettes.
- Map-scale composition: the mini street slice tests whether modules remain coherent when placed side by side.

## Scalability Risks Tested

This proof tests the risks most likely to break the Phase 4 direction at scale:

- Template-clone risk: repeated sign bands, awnings, and facade shells could make storefronts feel mechanically stamped.
- Fidelity-loss risk: decomposition could flatten the art into schematic parts and lose the rich raster atmosphere.
- Fictional-safe identity risk: abstract marks could become too decorative, too brand-like, or too close to accidental readable text.
- Prop-repetition risk: repeated plants, posters, boxes, and fixtures could reveal the module system in a bad way.
- Map-scale rhythm risk: variants could work as isolated boards but fail as neighbors in one street slice.
- UI attachment risk: marker, connector, card, and selected outline could only fit one bespoke storefront rather than a reusable family.
- Production-burden risk: rich raster personality may still require hand direction even if the system is modular.

## Where The System Held

The system holds best in material language and neighborhood cohesion:

- The variants read as plausible neighbors because they share brick, teal trim, warm cream panels, awning materials, inked edges, and sidewalk prop language.
- The recombination board shows clear reuse without reducing every variant to the same silhouette.
- The mini street slice preserves the Phase 4 map-scale atmosphere: storefront-led, warm, textured, and readable.
- Fictional-safe identity survives reuse better than blank signage because abstract glyphs give each storefront a distinct face without invoking real businesses.
- The selected-state and UI attachment proof shows the same marker/card treatment attached to a second variant, which suggests the UI is not purely one-off.
- The zoom crop makes reuse inspectable at the level Batu needs: sign identity, facade differentiation, prop repetition, clickable outline, and module seams.

## Where The System Broke Or Remains Weak

The system remains unproven in several places:

- The recombination board includes explanatory labels as review scaffolding. They help the proof read, but future style-direction artifacts should reduce visible label dependence.
- The shared sign-band grammar is strong enough to unify the set, but it may become too recognizable if too many storefronts use the same horizontal cream-panel structure.
- Abstract glyphs are fictional-safe in intent, but future production work would need accidental-text and accidental-brand review.
- Prop reuse works at this batch size, but a larger street would need a stronger strategy for spacing, silhouette, and density variation.
- The selected outline reads clearly, but it may be too bright for final visual language.
- The paper card system travels across variants, but its scale, content hierarchy, and interaction behavior remain review-only.
