# Direction Synthesis

Label: **lo-fi / exploratory / not final**  
Date: 2026-05-26  
Status: proposed directions for Batu reaction, not final art direction

## Context

Territory A, Storefront Sticker Diorama, is paused as a working hypothesis. It should not be approved or rejected from the earlier prose and Batch 4 artifacts alone.

These revised directions are grounded in the Batch 5 moodboard. They are meant to create useful contrast for Batu, not to settle the final visual language.

## Direction 1 - Community Sim Storefront

Visual thesis:
- A Greenpoint exterior storefront diorama with OCCII's community-sim confidence translated into labels, markers, and place-card hierarchy.
- The world stays local and architectural; the UI carries the flyer/social energy.

Reference cluster:
- OCCII Community Drinks flyer.
- RollerCoaster Tycoon Classic.
- Habbo.
- Peter Pan Donuts.
- Greenpoint Historic District.

World treatment:
- Pixel-inspired HD storefronts with chunky proportions.
- Strong sign bands, awnings, subway entrance, sidewalk clutter, older brick and vinyl surfaces.
- Ambient details feel social but not systemic: light, steam, small street objects.

UI treatment:
- Medium-loud sticker markers.
- Thick white/navy label treatment inspired by OCCII but not copied.
- Place card feels like a community flyer crossed with a factual place card.
- Green attention markers may inspire shape/color, but avoid plumbob duplication.

Palette/texture implications:
- World: brick, cream, asphalt blue-gray, aged green, bakery warm yellow.
- UI: navy, white, acid green, small red/blue accents.
- Texture mostly in world surfaces and card edges; avoid full poster overprint.

Risks:
- Could still become too close to OCCII if type and markers are over-literal.
- Could imply a social sim or NPC system if markers feel character-based.
- Could let UI overpower the storefront world.

Questions for Batu:
- Does the OCCII energy belong mostly in UI/card treatment rather than the map art?
- Is thick white/navy type a good influence, or too close to the flyer?
- Should social markers be abstract attention cues, or avoided entirely for MVP?

## Direction 2 - Printed Neighborhood Toy

Visual thesis:
- A compact Greenpoint diorama treated like a risograph neighborhood toy: local storefronts, bold print texture, sticker/card overlays, and a handmade community-map feel.

Reference cluster:
- Secret Riso Club Bushwick signs.
- Pen Pal Riso.
- Booklyn poster set.
- eBoy.
- Aly Miller Brooklyn map.

World treatment:
- Buildings remain isometric and local, but surfaces can have print-like flat colors, slight offset edges, and selective texture.
- Signage and street objects feel collected, like neighborhood ephemera.
- Diorama edges may feel like a printed cutout or tabletop object.

UI treatment:
- Stickers, tape tabs, and small poster-card overlays.
- Place cards lean more zine/flyer than game HUD.
- Markers can be imperfect, pasted, and tactile.

Palette/texture implications:
- Limited riso-like palette: cream paper, black ink, brick red, green, cobalt or medium blue, one fluorescent accent.
- Visible misregistration/dither used sparingly.
- Print texture should support charm, not reduce click clarity.

Risks:
- Could become too Bushwick-coded.
- Could flatten the scene into a poster instead of an explorable diorama.
- Could make local places feel like graphic motifs rather than real businesses.

Questions for Batu:
- Should the project feel handmade/printed, or more like a playable sim screenshot?
- How much riso texture is charming before it becomes noisy?
- Does this direction feel too far from Greenpoint architecture?

## Direction 3 - Civic Pixel Guide

Visual thesis:
- A cleaner, map-adjacent Greenpoint diorama that borrows from pixel sims and civic wayfinding, with restrained DIY accents for charm.

Reference cluster:
- SimCity 2000.
- Isometric NYC.
- NPS map symbols.
- NPS pictographic symbols.
- Greenpoint Historic District.
- Manhattan Avenue street sign.

World treatment:
- More disciplined geometry and clearer building/street relationships.
- Lower visual noise.
- Storefronts are simplified but accurate in placement and sign hierarchy.

UI treatment:
- Compact markers and label chips.
- Place card prioritizes factual clarity with one playful sticker accent.
- Less OCCII/flyer influence; more neighborhood-guide legibility.

Palette/texture implications:
- Warm civic palette: cream, brick, muted green, asphalt gray, MTA green, black/white.
- One bright accent only.
- Minimal texture, used for place specificity rather than atmosphere.

Risks:
- Could become too tasteful or utility-map-like.
- Could lose the core "worth clicking" delight.
- Could underuse the OCCII reference and feel emotionally cooler.

Questions for Batu:
- Is restraint appealing, or does it undershoot the desired energy?
- Should this project feel closer to a guide/map, or clearly like a playful sim diorama?
- What minimum amount of flyer/sticker language is needed to keep it alive?

## Current Recommendation From Codex

No final recommendation yet.

Based on the moodboard, Codex's working read is:

- Direction 1 best preserves the original emotional spark from OCCII while keeping Greenpoint exterior-first.
- Direction 2 may be the most distinctive if Batu wants stronger DIY print culture.
- Direction 3 is safest for clarity but may be too polite for the project's desired discovery emotion.

Batu should react to the moodboard before any direction becomes approved.
