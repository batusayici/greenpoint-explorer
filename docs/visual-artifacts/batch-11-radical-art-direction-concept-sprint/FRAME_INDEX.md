## Observed Frame Notes

### 1. Soft Pixel Neighborhood Sim

The frame is the strongest immediate product-fit candidate. Storefronts, sidewalks, marker logic, street furniture, and sign hierarchy are readable. It feels clickable and lively without heavy UI clutter. The risk is that it needs sharper differentiation from generic pixel neighborhood charm and from earlier Community Pixel adjacent work.

### 2. Xerox-Riso Street Zine

The frame is materially distinct and culturally loud. It succeeds at the "living bulletin board" idea. It is weaker as a product hero because the place-card logic is more poster insert than usable card, and the collage texture competes with click targets.

### 3. Tilt-Shift Toy Block Greenpoint

The frame is polished and physically appealing. The marker feels native to the diorama. The drawback is that Greenpoint specificity is lighter than the toy material, and the frame risks looking like a friendly generic miniature city product.

### 4. Inked Indie Graphic Novel

The frame has the strongest authored storefront specificity and a strong street-corner reading. It feels local, dense, and tasteful while preserving entrances and sidewalk logic. Its main risk is scale: the look needs a repeatable illustration system, not only a beautiful one-off.

### 5. Civic Atlas Deluxe

The frame is elegant and usable, with strong map clarity. It is the easiest to imagine as a clean product system, but it has the weakest cultural charge. It risks feeling like a premium city guide rather than a distinctive clickable diorama.

### 6. Weird Hyperlocal Magical Realism

The frame is memorable and emotionally rich, but it overshoots the brief in places. The moon, luminous props, and waterfront surrealism pull toward fantasy/mood art. It should be mined for controlled lighting and atmosphere, not advanced unchanged.
