## Observed Read

Soft Pixel Neighborhood Sim is the strongest MVP-friendly emotional/game readability candidate from the generated frames. It clearly supports map browsing and clickability, but it must still prove separation from prior pixel-adjacent work.

Xerox-Riso Street Zine is one of the most materially divergent outputs. It succeeds as a cultural surface and fails partially as a product frame: the card/marker logic reads more like poster insert and arrow than a durable browsing system.

Tilt-Shift Toy Block Greenpoint has a strong physical-world metaphor and could scale through modular 3D thinking. The generated frame is polished, but the Greenpoint specificity is not as strong as the miniature material.

Inked Indie Graphic Novel produces the strongest authored local specificity. The generated frame remains readable enough for map mode and carries strong storefront identity. Its risk is production consistency and repeatability.

Civic Atlas Deluxe is the most feasible and UI-compatible, but the generated frame is also the most vulnerable to becoming a polished city-guide plate rather than a distinctive clickable diorama.

Weird Hyperlocal Magical Realism is emotionally distinct and memorable, but the generated frame overshoots into fantasy/mood-art risk. It should influence lighting and atmosphere only if Batu wants that accent later.

## Thumbnail Divergence Expectations

At 20% zoom, the six options separate this way:

- Soft Pixel Neighborhood Sim: crisp chunky pixel neighborhood.
- Xerox-Riso Street Zine: paper, ink, tape, photocopy, and riso collage.
- Tilt-Shift Toy Block Greenpoint: miniature 3D toy diorama.
- Inked Indie Graphic Novel: hand-drawn inked city panel.
- Civic Atlas Deluxe: premium editorial atlas plate.
- Weird Hyperlocal Magical Realism: glowing dusk neighborhood myth.

The six hero frames now satisfy the divergence test well enough for survivor selection.
