## Territory 1: Soft Pixel Neighborhood Sim

Visual thesis: A cozy HD pixel / near-isometric neighborhood browser where Greenpoint storefronts feel handmade, legible, warm, and playable without becoming retro nostalgia.

Emotional promise: "I want to wander this block and click every little shop."

Rendering medium: High-resolution pixel-inspired raster painting with crisp stepped edges, subtle subpixel lighting, and readable chunky forms. Avoid strict low-res pixel art.

Camera / framing: Fixed isometric or near-isometric three-quarter view over one compact corner or storefront row, with enough street and sidewalk for markers and a place card.

Building shape language: Chunky brick and vinyl storefront boxes, clear sign bands, simplified cornices, stacked windows, awnings, roll gates, stoops, and corner geometry.

Storefront / signage language: Big readable store signs, hand-painted secondary notices, awnings, menu boards, window decals, small Polish / neighborhood cues where truth-safe.

Texture / material language: Crisp brick clusters, warm window glow, tiled sidewalk blocks, stickered poles, bike racks, newspaper boxes, and small pixel-clean props.

UI integration strategy: Markers feel like bright tactile stickers or pixel pins locked to storefront entrances. The place card uses chunky pixel-panel framing, but remains cleaner than the world.

How Bushwick / Greenpoint creative subculture appears in-world: Zine flyers on poles, bikes and e-bikes, tote-bag silhouettes, hand-drawn event posters, layered stickers, sidewalk sandwich boards, small craft-table hints, and expressive but readable signage.

What would make this direction fail: Becoming generic retro city art, too cute, too low-res, too game-HUD-heavy, or dependent on labels instead of storefront character.

Production feasibility risks: Pixel-inspired raster style can become expensive if every storefront needs custom authored detail. It needs a strict asset language for scalable facades and UI.

One-sentence image-generation prompt: Exterior-only near-isometric Greenpoint Brooklyn storefront corner in cozy HD pixel-art style, warm readable facades, street furniture, clickable place markers, and one clean pixel-inspired place card overlay.

Detailed image-generation prompt: Create an exterior-only near-isometric map browsing style frame for Greenpoint, Brooklyn: a compact sidewalk corner with brick storefront facades, awnings, sign bands, roll gates, bike rack, utility pole with stickers, street furniture, curb and crosswalk edge. Render as cozy HD pixel-inspired art with crisp chunky geometry, readable storefront silhouettes, warm modern neighborhood color, subtle window glow, and high clarity at thumbnail size. Include clear place markers pinned to storefront entrances and one place card overlay for a selected cafe or shop. The UI should feel integrated as pixel-sticker map affordances, not a full game HUD. Make the scene local, walkable, and exterior-only; do not copy any real reference image literally.

Negative prompt: interiors, cutaway rooms, inside venues, dialogue scene, fantasy village, cyberpunk, generic city, low-resolution nostalgia, blurry pixels, photorealism, abstract poster, full game HUD, quests, XP, character simulator, copied storefront, unreadable labels, excessive clutter.

Review questions for Batu:

- Does this feel warm and clickable without becoming generic retro?
- Is the HD pixel language charming enough to own the project?
- Are storefronts still the main event, not the UI?
- Does this have enough radical separation from the older Community Pixel path?

## Territory 2: Xerox-Riso Street Zine

Visual thesis: A navigable exterior map made from photocopy grain, risograph layers, sticker slabs, tape, wheatpaste, and DIY flyer logic, while still preserving storefront click targets.

Emotional promise: "The neighborhood feels like a living bulletin board I can explore."

Rendering medium: Raster collage with photocopy texture, risograph spot-color overprint, rough cut-paper edges, scanned ink, stickers, tape shadows, and imperfect registration.

Camera / framing: Near-isometric street-corner collage, with facades assembled from paper shapes and pasted signage. The map must remain spatial, not flatten into a poster.

Building shape language: Facades as stacked paper blocks, simplified perspective planes, torn-edge awnings, photocopied windows, pasted brick patterns, and bold entry shapes.

Storefront / signage language: Hand-lettered signs, taped flyers, screenprinted placards, sticker labels, wheatpaste panels, and messy-but-intentional wayfinding.

Texture / material language: Paper grain, copier noise, ink bleed, risograph misregistration, tape, peeled stickers, staple marks, scuffed black ink, and two-to-four strong spot colors.

UI integration strategy: Markers are sticker tabs, dot labels, or taped arrows. The place card is a layered flyer clipped to the map with a shadow, but its text hierarchy remains clean.

How Bushwick / Greenpoint creative subculture appears in-world: Show community-board energy through zine stacks, gig flyers, maker posters, protest-style sticker layering, art-market notices, bike delivery traces, and hand-modified shop signs.

What would make this direction fail: Becoming an abstract poster, losing navigable street depth, overloading texture until click targets disappear, or using "grit" as generic coolness.

Production feasibility risks: This is visually distinctive but may be hard to systematize for many places. Accessibility and label clarity need special care.

One-sentence image-generation prompt: Exterior-only near-isometric Greenpoint storefront map built as a Xerox-risograph street zine collage with navigable facades, sticker markers, and a layered flyer-style place card.

Detailed image-generation prompt: Create an exterior-only near-isometric map browsing frame for Greenpoint, Brooklyn, as a rough Xerox-risograph street zine collage. Show a sidewalk corner with storefront facades, awnings, street signs, utility pole, bike rack, newspaper box, curb, and crosswalk edge. Build the world from torn paper, photocopy grain, risograph misregistration, taped flyers, wheatpaste layers, stickers, and scanned ink textures. It must still read as a usable map with clear storefront doors, clickable markers, and one selected place card overlay designed like a layered community flyer. Keep the composition spatial and browsable, not poster-only. Do not copy any literal reference image.

Negative prompt: interior scene, venue cutaway, abstract poster only, flat moodboard, fantasy town, cyberpunk neon, generic graffiti alley, unreadable chaos, illegible UI, copied poster art, photorealism, clean vector isometric, low-effort grunge filter.

Review questions for Batu:

- Does the zine materiality feel like a world, not just a graphic skin?
- Can the user still browse storefronts and understand click targets?
- Is the DIY culture specific and warm, or too generic gritty?

## Territory 3: Tilt-Shift Toy Block Greenpoint

Visual thesis: A tactile miniature Greenpoint block built from chunky toy-like geometry, soft shadows, shallow depth of field, and collectible diorama charm.

Emotional promise: "I want to pick up the block, rotate it, and inspect every storefront."

Rendering medium: Stylized 3D / clay-render / low-poly miniature raster frame with soft global illumination, matte toy materials, small bevels, and tilt-shift optics.

Camera / framing: Elevated isometric or near-isometric tilt-shift view looking at one corner module, with storefronts, sidewalks, street furniture, and an anchored place-card layer.

Building shape language: Chunky toy storefront blocks, simplified cornices, soft bevels, thick awnings, compressed windows, tactile signs, and clean modular bases.

Storefront / signage language: Dimensional sign plaques, tiny printed decals, blocky awnings, storefront icons, miniature stickers, and readable shopfront symbols.

Texture / material language: Matte plastic, painted wood, clay, soft felt awnings, tiny paper decals, shallow scratches, soft shadows, and clean color separation.

UI integration strategy: Markers feel like small physical pins, map pegs, acrylic tabs, or museum-label flags inserted into the diorama. The place card floats like a clean exhibit label.

How Bushwick / Greenpoint creative subculture appears in-world: Tiny zine tables, bikes, stickered utility boxes, handmade shop signs, painted roll gates, mini poster walls, sidewalk planters, and art-market cues rendered as toy details.

What would make this direction fail: Looking like generic mobile-game toy town, losing Greenpoint specificity, becoming too cute, or making the UI feel like detached SaaS panels.

Production feasibility risks: 3D asset production may be attractive for scale but requires modeling discipline, lighting consistency, and careful avoidance of sterile asset-pack polish.

One-sentence image-generation prompt: Exterior-only tilt-shift miniature Greenpoint storefront corner as a tactile low-poly toy diorama with physical pin markers and one elegant place label.

Detailed image-generation prompt: Create an exterior-only near-isometric tilt-shift style frame of a Greenpoint, Brooklyn storefront corner as a tactile miniature diorama. Show chunky brick and vinyl storefront facades, awnings, curb, crosswalk edge, bike rack, utility pole, streetlight, planters, and shop signage. Render in stylized low-poly / clay / painted-wood materiality with soft shadows, small bevels, shallow depth of field, and collectible toy-block charm. Include physical map pins or acrylic tabs as place markers and one clean place-card overlay that feels like a small exhibit label. Keep it recognizably urban and local, not fantasy, suburban, or generic mobile-game town.

Negative prompt: interiors, cutaway rooms, toy castle, fantasy village, generic low-poly town, sterile mobile game, overly cute pastel world, photorealism, cyberpunk, abstract poster, copied real storefront, UI dashboard, no storefronts.

Review questions for Batu:

- Does the tactile miniature world feel emotionally right for Greenpoint browsing?
- Is the toy-like simplification charming or too cute?
- Could this scale into a map without becoming asset-pack generic?
- Does the physical marker system feel useful and distinctive?

## Territory 4: Inked Indie Graphic Novel

Visual thesis: An authored hand-drawn exterior city scene where Greenpoint storefronts feel specific, lived-in, and narrative through ink linework, expressive shadows, and local facade quirks.

Emotional promise: "Every storefront looks like it has a story, but the scene still works as a map."

Rendering medium: Hand-drawn ink and digital color raster frame with confident linework, expressive hatching, selective flat color, shadow shapes, and printed-comic texture.

Camera / framing: Near-isometric illustrated street corner, slightly less rigid than classic game isometric, with visible sidewalk/street logic and a selected storefront card.

Building shape language: Irregular facade proportions, hand-drawn brick rhythms, warped but controlled perspective, expressive sign bands, stoops, bay windows, and imperfect street edges.

Storefront / signage language: Lettered shop signs, layered window notes, hand-painted awnings, uneven decals, quirky facade details, and sign hierarchy that shows individual storefront identity.

Texture / material language: Ink hatching, brush shadows, paper grain, muted spot color, scuffed windows, old brick, roll gates, scratched metal, and expressive ambient marks.

UI integration strategy: Markers are inked callout pins or margin annotations that still map precisely to entrances. The place card feels like an illustrated caption panel with restrained comic framing.

How Bushwick / Greenpoint creative subculture appears in-world: Drawn flyers, stickered poles, artists' signage, delivery bikes, sidewalk racks, zine posters, hand-lettered notices, and small expressive street scenes without dialogue bubbles.

What would make this direction fail: Becoming a character scene instead of a place browser, overusing comic effects, losing click precision, or drifting into generic indie-city melancholy.

Production feasibility risks: High authorship is difficult to scale. Consistency across many storefronts may require a strong illustration pipeline or human art direction.

One-sentence image-generation prompt: Exterior-only near-isometric Greenpoint storefront map rendered as an inked indie graphic novel scene with precise markers and one caption-like place card.

Show a compact street corner with storefront facades, awnings, roll gates, sidewalk, curb, crosswalk edge, utility pole, bike rack, street furniture, stickers, and locally specific storefront quirks. Use confident hand-drawn ink linework, expressive hatching, printed paper texture, warm restrained color, and authored shadows. Include precise place markers as inked callouts attached to storefront entrances and one selected place card overlay that feels like a clean caption panel. Keep the scene browsable and exterior-only; no interior cutaways or dialogue scenes.

Negative prompt: interiors, character dialogue, speech balloons, cutaway rooms, fantasy city, cyberpunk, generic comic metropolis, abstract poster, overdramatic noir, unreadable dense hatching, photorealism, copied storefront.

Review questions for Batu:

- Does hand-authored linework make Greenpoint feel more specific?

Storefront / signage language: Minimal but warm signs, refined iconography, restrained awnings, curated window detail, address cues, and readable category markers.

Texture / material language: Subtle paper grain, ink-like strokes, muted local color, soft building shadows, clean pavement pattern, and restrained material accents.
