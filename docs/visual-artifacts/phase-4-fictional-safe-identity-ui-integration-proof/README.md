## What Changed From Phase 3.9

Phase 3.9 proved that the map-scale street slice could hold 4 fictional storefronts, but blank signs reduced personality and cultural specificity. Its hover/card pressure was legible, but the card felt visually under-integrated.

Phase 4 changes the test in two focused ways:

- Storefront sign bands now use fictional-safe visual identity: abstract glyphs, symbolic marks, hand-painted panels, sticker clusters, poster clusters, awning symbols, color blocking, and window/door motifs.
- The hover/card pressure test uses a more world-compatible marker, connector, and paper card with worn texture, hand-inked edges, muted color blocks, and abstract symbol placeholders.

The proof keeps the Phase 3.9 baseline: compact isometric street-slice composition, 4 fictional storefronts, warm hand-inked HD pixel-inspired raster rendering, sidewalk/curb/crosswalk context, utility/street clutter, and storefront-led readability.

## Fictional-Safe Signage Strategy

The signs restore personality without relying on readable names or factual claims.

Allowed identity devices used:

- Abstract icon systems.
- Partial glyphs that do not form words.
- Painted geometric motifs.
- Awning marks.
- Sticker and poster clusters.
- Symbolic door/window decals.
- Color-blocked sign panels.
- Weathered hand-painted forms.

Truth-risk controls:

- No real business names.
- No real brand references.
- No readable addresses.
- No exact real facade references.
- No exact station geometry.
- No factual card copy.
- No pasted labels.
- No production naming system.

Important caveat: generated micro-marks on posters, stickers, utility boxes, and card surfaces are treated as non-factual texture only. They must not be reused as production content.

## UI / World Integration Notes

The hover/card pressure proof tests a selected middle storefront with:

- A warm selected-region outline that follows the storefront mass.
- A marker that reads closer to a printed tag or enamel/paper symbol than a generic map pin.
- An ink-dot connector.
- A worn paper card with taped corner, hand-inked edge, abstract symbol panel, muted placeholder blocks, and soft world shadow.

Compared with Phase 3.9, the card is more compatible with the storefront art because it shares paper texture, color restraint, inked edges, and symbolic content. It is still review-only and does not approve production marker/card language.

Remaining UI risks:

- The card is more integrated but still occupies a large lower-frame area.
- The selected outline is readable, but it introduces glow/attention pressure against dense storefront art.
- The marker/card system needs later product and interaction review before implementation.

## Production / Scalability Caveats

The proof makes fictional-safe identity more convincing, but it also exposes production questions:

- Abstract signage may need hand direction to avoid accidental real-looking words or brand-like marks.
- Each storefront identity needs enough uniqueness to feel local without becoming bespoke illustration overhead.
- Rich raster texture remains attractive but production-intensive.
- Generated micro-details require review before any production use.
- UI integration looks promising visually, but this is not an implementation, asset pipeline, or interaction-system approval.
