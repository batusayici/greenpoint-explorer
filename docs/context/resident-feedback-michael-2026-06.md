# Resident Feedback — Michael (June 2026)

> Emerging context from neighbor interviews. Source: "Local resident feedback (Michael).pdf".
> Status: raw signal, not yet adopted into PLAN.md. Treat as product-direction input.

## Headline insight
The map is the **container**; the **hyperlocal context attached to places** is the product.
The strongest differentiator is NOT the map itself — it's the stories, people, history,
events, and neighborhood knowledge layered onto it.

## Emerging product framework — three layers
- **Layer 1 — Place** (static foundation): buildings, architecture, landmarks, streets,
  historic district. Purpose: visual identity + differentiation. *(This is what we're
  currently building.)*
- **Layer 2 — Stories** (hyperlocal content): local audio stories, historical photos,
  owner stories, neighborhood memories. Purpose: uniqueness + defensibility.
- **Layer 3 — What's Happening Now** (dynamic): stoop sales, events, news, community
  updates, business happenings. Purpose: repeat visitation.

## Key takeaways
1. **Business-owner pride is a distribution lever.** Owners are emotionally attached to
   being represented accurately (seeing their storefront rendered, featured alongside
   landmarks, sharing their story). Participation driven by pride/identity more than ROI.
   → Future feature: owner profile, owner audio intro, business story, team photos.
   *(Connects to our [[claim-monetization-model]] — pride may be the real hook, not branding.)*
2. **Personality > directory info.** Weak reactions to hours/addresses/listings. Strong
   reactions to owner stories, local history, origin stories, neighborhood lore.
   "People become attached to people, not listings." Examples named: DMP Shipping,
   Fusion Bites, local hardware store, pizza shop owner.
3. **Landmark audio stories validate strongly.** Concepts: The Corn Building, The Haunted
   House, Astral Apartments, Greenpoint Savings Bank, Bushwick Inlet Park. Interviewee
   volunteered to record on location — "I want it on the steps." Place-based storytelling
   feels authentic; local experts are willing contributors.
4. **Stoop sales = strong discovery use case.** Generated immediate enthusiasm (more than
   historical content). Layer: stoop sales, yard sales, block parties, community gatherings.
   Frequent updates → recurring traffic driver.
5. **Local news layer resonated.** Attach neighborhood news to physical locations (Bushwick
   Inlet Park updates, development projects, community meetings, park advocacy, openings/
   closures). Potential partners: Greenpointers, Save the Inlet, community orgs.
6. **Community orgs may be better early partners than businesses.** Save the Inlet, Bushwick
   Inlet advocates, walking-tour operators, local historians, neighborhood experts already
   produce knowledge/stories/events/history — better early content + distribution source.
7. **User-generated layers concept.** People perceive the explorer as a *platform*, not just
   a map: Dogs of Greenpoint, dating, community causes, local interest groups, projects.

## Growth & acquisition
- Businesses are NOT the initial traffic source. Sequence: (1) attract visitors first,
  (2) demonstrate engagement, (3) bring businesses onboard.
- Early distribution targets once MVP is populated: Save the Inlet, Bushwick Inlet advocates,
  local historians, walking-tour operators, Greenpointers, historic preservation groups,
  longtime residents.

## Open questions
- **What creates the first 100 recurring users?** Candidates: landmark stories, stoop sales,
  community events, local news, park advocacy updates.
- **Business value prop:** compelling = identity, storytelling, community connection, enhanced
  profiles, event promotion, brand visibility. Less compelling = generic directory listings,
  basic coupons.
