## 🔴 Finding 2 — ten new visitors arrived, opened almost nothing, and are untagged

Since cycle 2 fired (2026-08-03 00:00 UTC), production recorded:

| Event | Taps | People |
|---|---|---|
| `return_visit` | 14 | 12 |
| `$pageview` | 13 | 10 |
| `card_open` | **2** | **1** |
| `filter_tap` | 2 | 2 |
| `alert_tap` | 1 | 1 |

**10 people appeared for the first time** (7 on Aug 3, 3 on Aug 4) — the largest
two-day influx since Jul 22. Every one of them: `$direct` referrer, no `src`
tag, landed on `/`, never went deeper. Seven of them fired `return_visit` with
`visitCount = 1` on Aug 3 — **fresh `localStorage`**, meaning genuinely fresh
browser profiles or incognito windows, not PostHog's cookieless IDs rotating.

There are exactly two explanations and **the data cannot separate them:**

1. **It is our own cutover verification.** Aug 3 is the day the new origin was
   checked. Six events *were* tagged `src=verify` and excluded — but if the same
   session also opened untagged tabs or incognito windows, those land in every
   metric permanently. Fresh-localStorage-on-the-verification-day fits this
   exactly.
2. **They are real post-cutover arrivals** — in which case 10 new visitors
   produced 2 card opens between them, and that is by far the worst activation
   reading in the product's history and would be the headline of this readout.

**The cost is already booked either way.** These 10 people are in the
denominator of every rate below:

| Metric | Cycle 2 | Today | Change driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Activation rate | 7 of 28 (25%) | **7 of 38 (18%)** | numerator unchanged; +10 denominator |
| Card-open conversion | 10 of 23 (43%) | **10 of 32 (31%)** | numerator unchanged; +9 visitors |
| Multi-day return | 9 of 28 (32%) | **9 of 38 (24%)** | numerator unchanged |

**Not one of those numerators moved.** Three headline rates fell by a quarter
without a single existing user changing behavior — and if explanation 1 is
right, they fell because of a link we opened ourselves.

**Recommendation (Batu's call): (a) confirm whether the Aug 3–4 traffic was
yours, and (b) adopt one rule — every production check opens the `?src=verify`
link from `channel-links.md`, always, including incognito and second-device
checks.** The row already exists and the pull already excludes it; the only
missing piece is the habit. An untagged staff visit is indistinguishable from a
real one and stays in the demand-gate denominator forever — the same permanence
that made the friends round unattributable.

This is the **third distinct measurement defect in three cycles** (untagged
invites → dev traffic in prod → untagged internal traffic). All three have the
same shape: measurement is degraded by our own actions, discovered afterwards,
and never recoverable.

## Top-3 proposals

**No new experiment proposed.** R1/Q1/Q2 hold all three slots the moment Wave 1
fires (growth-engine §6 rule 3). All three below are measurement and sequencing
work; #1 and #2 are gates rather than experiments.

### 1. Tag every production check — `?src=verify`, without exception

- **Hypothesis:** the third measurement defect in three cycles has the same
  shape as the first two, and it is the cheapest one to close. Ten untagged
  visitors moved three headline rates by a quarter in 36 hours; if they were
  ours, that is a self-inflicted wound on the exact denominator the demand gate
  reads.
- **Smallest test:** no build. One habit — every prod visit for verification,
  spot-checking, or demoing opens
  `https://greenpoint.life/?src=verify` from `channel-links.md`, including
  incognito windows and second devices. Optionally one sentence added to
  launch-plan §2 step 5 and to the `channel-links.md` pre-send checklist.
- **Metric:** untagged first-appearance bursts per cycle.
- **Decision rule:** if cycle 4 still shows same-day fresh-`localStorage`
  untagged bursts, the habit isn't holding → stop relying on it and add a
  documented exclusion (e.g. exclude `$pageview`-only sessions with zero
  interaction from activation denominators), which is worse but mechanical.
- **Kill criteria:** n/a — a hygiene rule, not an experiment.
- **Immediate ask (only Batu can answer):** was the Aug 3–4 traffic yours? The
  answer changes whether Finding 2 is a measurement note or the most important
  activation signal we have.

### 2. Wave 1 waits for a post-fix ingest run — rule carried forward verbatim

- **Hypothesis:** seeding is one-shot per trust network (echo-chamber targeting,
  growth-engine §4), and the feed is 21% below baseline on dated in-window items
  with a fifth of the roster yielding. The 8/3 fixes are the repair; **no run
  has exercised them.**
- **Smallest test:** after the next **full** ingest run lands, re-run
  `node scripts/check-freshness.mjs` and record `datedUpcoming7d`.
- **Metric:** `datedUpcoming7d` at send time, recorded in the readout.
- **Decision rule (unchanged from cycle 2, deliberately not softened):** ≥35 →
  send Wave 1. 27–34 → send, but the org notes' "this week" framing carries less
  weight. **<27 after a full post-fix run → the decline is supply decay, not the
  weekend, and that is a roster problem to fix before spending trust networks on
  it.**
- **Kill criteria:** n/a — sequencing.
- **Note:** cycle 2 set this rule against a *pre*-fix run, which could not have
  passed it. The first honest read is the first run after `a0aa00a`.

### 3. Give `check-freshness.mjs` a trend check, not just a floor

- **Hypothesis:** the instrument that is supposed to catch supply decay reported
  **FRESH** through a 21% two-week decline, because `thinFeed` is an absolute
  floor of 10 against a deck of 75. It measures the floor, not the trend — the
  defect that let the decline run silently (supply-analysis, observability gap,
  item 8).
- **Smallest test:** stamp `datedUpcoming7d` per run and flag a week-over-week
  decline beyond a threshold; keep the existing floor as-is.
- **Metric:** does the alarm fire on a real decline before a readout notices it?
- **Decision rule:** if the trend check fires on ordinary weekend troughs (the
  27 → 38 → 27 sawtooth is normal), it is measuring the wrong window → compare
  same-weekday-to-same-weekday instead of run-to-run.
- **Kill criteria:** two false alarms in four weeks → drop it; a noisy alarm is
  worse than none (the same rule cycle 2 applied to the pull's preflight).
- **Autonomy note:** code change → **V2, PR-gated, TDD, and not in this PR**
  (this PR is a readout). It is also arguably ingest-side rather than
  growth-side; raised here because the growth readout is what it protects.
