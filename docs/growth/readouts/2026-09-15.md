## 🟡 Finding 2 — The demand gate reads 7%, and the reason is that 670 people are four days old

This is the ~Sep 15 provisional demand readout that `growth-engine.md` has
pointed at since July. Here is the rule, and here is what it computes.

> *"of locals who visit twice, ≥25% return in ≥3 of any 4 consecutive weeks,
> floor 12 people"* — D1, re-registered 2026-08-17.

| Demand gate | 9/15 | 9/8 | 9/1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Locals with ≥2 distinct visit days (denominator) | **75** | 24 | 22 |
| Of those, in ≥3 of any 4 consecutive weeks (numerator) | **5** | 5 | 5 |
| **Gate rate** (bar ≥25%) | **7%** | 21% | 23% |
| **Floor** (bar ≥12 people) | **5** | 5 | 5 |
| WRL proxy (viability line, bar ≥30) | **11 people · 41 events** | 8 · 20 | 7 · 17 |

**Read it as arithmetic.** The numerator did not move. The denominator tripled,
because a person qualifies for it by visiting on two days — which 51 of the
wave's arrivals did within the same week — and qualifies for the numerator only
by being present in three separate calendar weeks, which requires having
existed for three weeks. Nobody who arrived on 9/11 can enter the numerator
before the week of 9/28 at the earliest.

**The same rule, restricted to people old enough to satisfy it:**

| Mature-cohort diagnostic — first visit on or before 2026-08-18 | Locals | All real people |
|---|---|---|
| People | 72 | 164 |
| With ≥2 distinct visit days | **18** | 24 |
| In ≥3 of any 4 consecutive weeks | **5** | 6 |
| Rate | **28%** | 25% |
| Floor (bar ≥12) | 5 | 6 |

**This is a diagnostic, not the gate, and it is not a softened rule** — it is
the same rule on the only population that could ever pass it today. On that
population the *rate* clears 25% and the *floor* does not: five people, against
twelve.

**What this hands Batu (recommendation marked).** The gate never said whose
history counts, because until Friday every visitor had roughly the same amount
of it. Now the population is 72% four days old, and the same rule produces 7%
or 28% depending on a question nobody has answered.

- **Recommended:** rule that the gate reads on a **maturity-qualified
  denominator** — people whose first visit is at least four weeks back, so
  everyone in the denominator has had the chance to enter the numerator — and
  that the whole-population rate stays reported beside it as the floor it is.
  This is the same shape as the existing plan: growth-engine already says the
  firm verdict waits for "two mature 4-week cohorts, ~late Oct".
- **The alternative** is to leave the rule as written and accept that the gate
  reads near zero until mid-October by construction. That is defensible — it is
  what pre-registration is for — but it should be a decision, not a side effect.
- **Either way, the floor of 12 is the binding constraint, not the rate.** Five
  people have returned in three separate weeks. The wave's first chance to add
  to that number is the week of 9/28, and the email that would bring them back
  has not gone out yet.

**Labels this read carries**, all pre-registered: broadcast-seed contamination
(P1 — Rana's posts started a fresh 4-week clock on 9/11, so organic share is
unreadable until ~10/9) · the September rebound confound (2a) · the
peak-cohort label (P8 — mature cohorts sitting wholly inside Sep–Oct are
provisional) · and the Safari label, which this week points the other way
(Finding 6).

**One more thing the population change did.** Growth-engine's gate clause says
"locals". On 2026-09-13 Batu ruled that the instrument counts **real people**
rather than local people, because geography was doing bot-filtering work that a
data-centre city list now does better. The gate above is still computed on
NY/NJ/CT (`$geoip_subdivision_1_name`), the same filter cycles 7–9 used, so its
trend is like-for-like. **Whether the gate keeps the "locals" cut is the second
half of the same ruling** and belongs with it.

## 🟡 Finding 6 — The Safari return gap inverted, because the wave arrived in Facebook's browser

| Return rate by browser family (28 days, from the instrument) | 9/15 | 9/14 | 9/13 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safari + Mobile Safari | **27 of 279 (10%)** | 22 of 267 (8%) | 16 of 258 (6%) |
| Everything else | **37 of 499 (7%)** | 34 of 481 (7%) | 23 of 456 (5%) |

For five readouts Safari returned at half the rate of everything else, and the
explanation was that Safari evicts the storage the return sensor writes. This
week Safari is *higher*. Nothing about Safari changed — the "everything else"
column did: it is now mostly people who arrived inside the Facebook in-app
browser, which fragments storage worse than Safari does and whose users landed
on one page and left.

**The mechanism is unchanged and still one-directional**: an evicted reader
re-registers as new, which inflates the gate's denominator and deflates its
numerator, so the true return rate is at or above what the gate shows, never
below. What flipped is which column carries the bigger undercount.

**Not comparable to cycle 9's version of this line** (Safari 10% vs other 22%):
that one was hand-computed on NY/NJ/CT locals over all history, this one is the
instrument's 28-day window over real people. Same name, different measurement.
