## 🟢 Finding 1 — ChatGPT sent us real local readers, and we had been reporting it as zero

On Saturday 2026-08-29, seven people arrived from ChatGPT: 43 events across 13
URLs, twelve of which were individual card pages.

| The ChatGPT visitors, 2026-08-29 | |
|---|---|
| People | **7** |
| Region / browser / device | **New York · Mobile Safari · mobile — all seven** |
| Card opens | **14, by 6 of the 7** |
| Landing pages | 13 (the home page plus 12 `/e/<slug>` card pages) |
| High-intent actions | 1 `action_tap`, 1 `related_tap`, 2 filter taps, 4 pin taps |
| Returned within the day | 2 of 7 |

Six of seven opened a card. Locals overall open a card at 29%. This is the
highest-converting arrival the project has ever recorded, and it is the exact
shape Loop C was built for: an assistant answering "what's happening in
Greenpoint" by citing dated, sourced card pages.

**Why every previous readout said zero.** The AI-referrer line was queried
against `$referring_domain`. ChatGPT strips the referrer — its visitors arrive
as `$direct` — and marks the link with `utm_source=chatgpt.com` instead. The
number was never zero; the question was never asked. Four consecutive readouts
(cycles 5, 6, 7 and the 8/11 one) published "AI-referrer sessions: 0, all-time
— zero, not missing." That sentence was wrong, and the correction matters
because P10 pre-registered AI referrals as their own decision-bearing line.

**Two honest caveats.**

- **This may be fewer than seven people.** PostHog here is cookieless, so
  identity rotates; seven ids on one day from the same region, browser and
  device could be fewer humans. Against that: the seven sessions are spread
  from 15:24 to 22:01, and two of them registered a second visit with an
  incremented visit count, which a single rotating identity would not produce.
- **It is one day.** No ChatGPT-tagged traffic before or after 8/29. One
  assistant answer, or one shared conversation, could account for all of it.

**Recommendation (Batu's call):** treat this as Q3's fourth positive read and
its first non-search evidence — recorded, not graduated, per the rule. And
approve the one-line instrument fix so the number is measured rather than
hand-queried each week (calibration log, instruction 1). Q3 has no kill rule
by design, so nothing here fires anything; what changes is that the loop
Batu has been funding on faith now has a first result.

## 🔴 Finding 2 — the first weekly note went out, and the link was broken for half the people who clicked it

Cycle 7 recommended sending the digest on 8/25. It went the same day. What
came back:

| `src` | People | Events each | Card opens | First / last event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `digest` | 8 | 2 | **0** | 8/25 / 8/25 |
| **`digest\`** *(a trailing backtick)* | **7** | 2 | **0** | 8/25 / 8/25 |

**Two things went wrong, and they are separate.**

**The link carried a backtick.** `digest\`` is not a channel — it is
`?src=digest` with a stray character attached, and it is not in
`channel-links.md`. Seven of fifteen arrivals, 47%, landed on a tag that
matches nothing. Cycle 7's draft printed the URL inside code formatting
(`` `https://stoopwise.com/?src=digest` ``) and one of the copies took the
closing backtick with it. The link itself still worked; only the attribution
broke. This is exactly the failure `channel-links.md` exists to prevent, one
step further upstream: the table was right, the way the readout presented it
was not.

**Most of the clicks were not people.** Of the 15 arrivals, **14 are in Oregon,
Iowa, Georgia and Texas** — one in New York. Each produced exactly two events
and opened nothing. That is the signature of mail-security link scanners
pre-fetching URLs, not readers. The one New York arrival (Chrome on iOS) also
opened no card.

**What that means for D9.** D9 judges the container question on
`?src=digest` **clicks**, and reads three consecutive sends against list size.
On this evidence, a raw click count would answer that question with scanner
traffic. The rule needs clicks that produced at least one in-product event
before it can be read honestly — see the calibration log.

**And the second send did not go.** D2's cadence is Monday. There are **zero
`?src=digest` events on or after 8/26**, so Mon Aug 31 produced nothing. Given
that the 8/25 send drew fifteen arrivals within a day, mostly automated, a
send with literally no arrivals is far more likely to be a send that did not
happen than a send nobody touched. The retention spine has now sent once in
its first two scheduled weeks.

**Recommendation (Batu's call):** send today, one day late, with the corrected
link. Draft is below, picks verified against `cards.json` at HEAD. Resume
Mondays Sep 14 — the Sep 7 send is already ruled to move to Tue Sep 8.
