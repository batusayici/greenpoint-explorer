# Stoopwise — Strategy One-Pager

*Last updated 2026-08-13. No pricing or revenue numbers here on purpose — those stay private. Full detail lives in `docs/growth/business-model.md`, `docs/growth/growth-engine.md`, `docs/PLAN.md`.*

## The problem

Greenpoint has a lot going on — events, new shops, deals, local news — but no single place to find all of it. It's scattered across newsletters, Instagram posts, and word of mouth, and by the time you hear about something it's often already out of date.

## What we're building

Stoopwise Greenpoint is a live map and feed of everything happening in the neighborhood, always kept current, free for anyone to use. It's paid for by local businesses and institutions who benefit from a lively, easy-to-navigate neighborhood — never by the people using it.

Most local newsletters pick a handful of highlights each week. That's useful once, then it goes stale. Stoopwise does the opposite: instead of picking highlights, we try to track everything, so it's still useful whenever you happen to check — Tuesday at 6pm, three weeks from now, whenever.

The brand is called **Stoopwise**; this is the Greenpoint edition, at **stoopwise.com**. If it works here, the plan is to repeat it in other neighborhoods (Astoria is next) — that repeatability is the real test of whether this is a business, not just a one-off project.

## How it works today

Every week, real local sources — newsletters, Instagram accounts, official event listings — get checked, and anything genuinely happening gets added to the map. Nothing is invented or guessed: if something can't be confirmed against a real source, it doesn't go up, or a person checks it by hand first.

A newer focus: making sure AI tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity can find Stoopwise and answer questions about Greenpoint correctly using it. More people ask AI assistants instead of searching or reading a newsletter, so being trustworthy to those tools is becoming as important as being trustworthy to a person browsing the site directly.

## How it makes money

The rule behind every dollar: businesses can pay for visibility or a helpful service, but never to get covered, and never to change what's true. Coverage of a business is always free and never for sale, and anything paid is always clearly labeled as an ad or a service.

Revenue is meant to arrive in three stages, each one earned by proof the last one works, not opened all at once:

1. **A small number of close relationships** with local institutions and businesses, sold on real value delivered — not on audience size, which is still small.
2. **Simple tools for local businesses to use themselves** — but only built once there's real proof businesses want them. The first version of this is just a person doing it by hand, before any software gets built.
3. **Deeper, paid analysis of neighborhood trends** for institutions who need that — a later idea, once the index itself is well-established and trusted.

What actually makes this valuable over time isn't the map itself — it's what builds up behind it: years of accurate coverage, real relationships with local businesses, proof that a listing actually brought a business customers, and a growing base of residents who follow and trust it. That's hard to copy quickly, and it gets more valuable the longer it runs honestly.

## How it grows

Right now, growth comes from people wanting to come back and tell others — no ads, no marketing budget. Three ways this compounds:

- **People come back** because the weekly content is genuinely useful, and they can follow specific places or topics they care about.
- **Local businesses and organizers pitch in** on their own, because it's worth their time to be listed accurately.
- **AI assistants start citing Stoopwise** as the source for what's happening in Greenpoint — essentially free, high-quality traffic.

We're deliberate about it: never running more than a few experiments at once, and always checking a clear before-and-after result rather than going on gut feel.

## Where things stand (2026-08-13)

The product is fully built and live at stoopwise.com. Right now we're watching real usage to answer four questions before doing more:

- **Do enough people come back regularly?** First real read ~Sep 15; firmer answer ~late October.
- **Are people finding and sharing it on their own**, through more than one way?
- **Are local businesses pitching in proactively**, without being asked?
- **Will businesses actually pay for something?** Goal: 3 real paid deals or firm commitments by Feb 15, 2027.

We can start talking to businesses now, but nothing gets sold until there's real proof people keep coming back. This week's work made the product more reliable (fixed a bug where one small failure could break the whole page) and more visible to AI tools — and an outside review this week largely agreed with the plan as it stands, which was a useful gut-check.

## Open questions

Will AI tools actually start sending people here? (We'll know more in about a month.) Will the "few close business relationships" approach actually pay off once tested for real, not just in theory? And longer-term: does this work in a second neighborhood, or was Greenpoint a special case?
